## Why small and medium companies overpay for hotels

Large companies negotiate annual hotel rates and audit them. Most small and medium companies do not have the volume for that, so they pay whatever the public price is on the day someone books.

### Public prices, searched from one country

Employees search from their own country, on one or two sites, and book. Different countries get access to different inventory, sales channels, local rates and promotions, so the same hotel is often 5-30% cheaper when searched from somewhere else. In a test of 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, 80% of countries showed a lower price than the traveler's own country, and the typical gap was 20-40%.

### Booked once, never checked again

Hotel prices keep moving after a booking is made. A refundable booking made three weeks out can often be rebooked for less a week later, but nobody on a small team has time to keep checking. When a lower price does appear, it often has to be taken at once, or someone else takes it.

### Trade show and conference weeks

Hotels near a venue raise prices for the dates of a big event, and teams tend to book late, after the event block has sold out. For many companies these are the most expensive hotel nights of the year. Our guide to [trade show hotel prices](https://arbitrica.com/blog/trade-show-hotel-prices.html) covers why and what to do about it.

### Nobody owns the number

Hotel nights are spread across expense reports and company cards. Procurement sees the total once a quarter, long after anything could have been done about a single booking.

## How Arbitrica works for a team

1. **The employee books as usual.** On the site they normally use, for the hotel and dates the trip needs.
2. **The agent shows up at the checkout.** Arbitrica's agent opens a chat next to the checkout, reads the price and confirms the hotel, dates, guests, room type, cancellation terms and breakfast.
3. **It searches from 195 countries.** It finds the lowest price in the world for the same hotel and validates it: identical hotel, all taxes and fees included, and a price that will not change at payment.
4. **It prepares everything.** The booking at that price opens in a new tab. The employee reviews it and completes the payment with the card they would have used anyway.
5. **It keeps going after booking.** With a refundable booking, Arbitrica keeps searching from 195 countries until the cancellation window ends. When the same hotel drops in price, it prepares a rebooking. The new booking is confirmed first, and only then is the original cancelled.

The confirmation comes from the seller that offered the lowest price, like any other hotel booking, so it goes into the expense report the usual way. See [how Arbitrica works](https://arbitrica.com/how-it-works.html) for the full flow.

## For procurement managers

Hotels are a hard category to buy well: no single supplier, prices that change every day and bookings made by dozens of people. Arbitrica does not ask you to sign a hotel contract or move bookings into a new channel. It works where the money is spent, on each booking, and shows the employee the price they were given next to the lowest price in the world, so the saving on every booking is visible at the moment it is made.

Four questions worth asking about any hotel savings approach, and how Arbitrica answers them:

- Is it the identical hotel, room and terms? Arbitrica validates the hotel, dates, guests, room type, cancellation terms and breakfast before it shows a price.
- Are the totals comparable? Prices are compared with all taxes and fees included.
- Can a traveler be left without a room? No. In a rebooking the new booking is confirmed before the original is cancelled.
- What happens to data? Arbitrica reads checkout pages only to find a lower price and does not sell personal data to third parties. See the [privacy policy](https://arbitrica.com/assets/privacy-policy.html).

## Trade shows and conferences

Event weeks are where a team's hotel bill is highest and where a lower price is worth the most. A few rules help any team:

- Book a refundable rate as soon as the dates are fixed, then keep checking before the cancellation deadline, which is often set in the hotel's local time.
- Compare the event block with the public price for the same hotel. The block is not always lower.
- Check the same hotel from other countries, not just other sites.
- Keep everyone in the same hotel if that matters for the team; a lower price for the same hotel is the goal, not a different hotel.
