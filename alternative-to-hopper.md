Hopper is a booking app built around price prediction and price freezes. It stops watching once you book, and it searches from one country. Arbitrica is a personal AI agent that lives in your browser and shows up at checkout, searches the same hotel from 195 countries, checks that the lower price is for the identical hotel with all taxes and fees included, and prepares the booking so you only review and pay. Its approach then repeats the search after you book, until the free-cancellation window ends.

## Why people search for a Hopper alternative

**Short answer:** Hopper made its name on flight-price prediction and grew into one of the largest travel-booking apps in North America. Two limits push people to look elsewhere: Hopper does not keep watching a reservation after it is booked, and it does not look at what the same hotel costs when searched from other countries.

Hopper's product is flight and hotel booking with predictive pricing. It tells you whether to book now or wait, sells Price Freeze, which holds a price for a fee (up to 60 days on hotels, with the protection capped; Hopper's launch announcement said it would cover up to $100 of an increase), and runs the Carrot Cash rewards program. Hopper also states that it does not price-match hotel reservations. If the rate for your hotel falls after you book, Hopper does not act on it.

## What does Arbitrica do that Hopper does not?

**Short answer:** It works on two kinds of arbitrage that Hopper does not touch.

- **Geographic arbitrage, live at checkout**Different countries get access to different inventory, sales channels, local rates and promotions. Searching the same hotel from another country can open up prices you cannot get from your own country. Doing that by hand with a VPN, one country at a time, works for three or four countries at most. Arbitrica searches from all 195.

- **Multiple-time arbitrage, after you book**Hotel rates keep moving after you pay. Arbitrica's approach repeats the search at many points in time until your free-cancellation window ends, and when it finds a lower price for the identical hotel it prepares the new booking. The new booking is confirmed before the original is cancelled.

In both cases the provider is not the point. The lower price comes from a different seller from the one you are on: Booking, Expedia, the hotel chain or anyone else. Arbitrica validates that it is the same hotel, the same dates and the same conditions, that taxes and fees are included, and that the price will not suddenly change at payment.

One real example: a hotel offered at $1,527.39 on Booking was found for $1,180.27. Arbitrica earned that traveler $347.12, or 23%, on a single stay.

## Prediction versus searching from 195 countries

A price prediction answers one question: is the price you see in your own market likely to go up or down? That is useful for flights, where one market and one fare matter. For hotels it leaves out the bigger gap. In a test of 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, 80% of countries showed a lower price than the traveler's own country, and the typical gap was 20-40%. A forecast built on one market cannot find that difference, however accurate it is.

The two approaches also treat timing differently. Price Freeze lets you pay to hold a price before you commit. A refundable booking does something similar without a separate fee: you hold the hotel, and if the price falls before the cancellation deadline you can book again at the lower price. That is the window Arbitrica's approach keeps working in. The guide to [what to do when a hotel price drops after booking](https://arbitrica.com/blog/hotel-price-drop-after-booking.html) covers the manual version.

## When is Hopper the better choice?

**Short answer:** Hopper is the better pick if you mostly book domestic flights on your phone and want guidance on when to buy, or if you collect Carrot Cash. Price Freeze is also useful for a trip you are fairly sure about but cannot commit to yet.

## When is Arbitrica the better choice?

**Short answer:** Arbitrica is the better pick for international hotels, and for anyone who books refundable. The same hotel is often 5 to 30% cheaper when searched from another country, and a forecast cannot find that gap because it only looks at one market. The repeated search after booking adds a second chance to pay less on the same reservation. Research showed that people earn on average $47 per hotel booking with Arbitrica, and up to $3,000 a year.

## What other Hopper alternatives exist?

**Short answer:** The category is fragmented, and most tools cover one piece.

- **Google Hotels and Google Flights** track prices and email you when they move. They do not rebook anything.

- **RatePunk** checks the same hotel on several booking sites before you book, with price alerts. It does not search from other countries.

- **RoomHawk** focuses on price alerts for a hotel after you book.

- **Pruvo** was the best-known post-booking hotel tracker until 2026, when it moved to business-only and stopped serving travelers. Arbitrica works for both: individual travelers, and companies whose teams travel to trade shows and conferences, along with their operations managers and travel managers.

## How do I switch from Hopper to Arbitrica?

**Short answer:** There is nothing to migrate. Hopper is an app with its own booking flow; Arbitrica is a personal AI agent that lives in your browser and appears at checkout when you book on the web. Keep Hopper on your phone for flight predictions if you like them. The two do not conflict.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will I lose my Hopper Carrot Cash if I use Arbitrica?

No. Arbitrica does not interact with your Hopper account. Any Carrot Cash you have collected stays available for redemption inside Hopper.

### Does Arbitrica predict hotel prices like Hopper does?

No. Hopper estimates whether a price will rise or fall. Arbitrica looks for the lowest price that exists right now by searching the same hotel from 195 countries, and its approach repeats that search after you book until the free-cancellation window ends. On hotels, the gap between countries is often larger than anything a forecast can win you.

### Can I use Arbitrica on the Hopper app itself?

No. Arbitrica is a personal AI agent that lives in your browser and works on web checkouts such as Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, Agoda and hotel chain sites. Bookings made inside the Hopper mobile app are not visible to it.

### Does Arbitrica only work for hotels?

No. Hotels are where the gap between countries is easiest to see, but the same mechanisms apply to flights, car rentals, event tickets, online shopping such as Amazon, and subscriptions: anything sold at different prices in different countries or with a cancellation or return window.
