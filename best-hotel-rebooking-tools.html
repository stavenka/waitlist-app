What rebooking actually means, and why most tools do not do it

Hotels reprice continuously. A property that is tracking behind its occupancy forecast for a Tuesday in February will discount into that Tuesday as it gets closer. If you are holding a refundable reservation, that price drop is money you can collect: you book the same hotel again at the lower rate and cancel the original. Nothing about that is a loophole. It is the flexibility you already paid for when you chose the refundable rate over the locked one.

The catch is that it only works if somebody keeps searching, every day, until the free-cancellation window ends. Almost nobody does this by hand for more than one trip. So the category splits into three tiers:

  • Alerting. Tells you the price moved. You do the rest. Google Hotels, RatePunk.
  • Pre-booking only. Watches until you commit, then stops, which is the wrong half of the timeline. Hopper.
  • Actual rebooking. Repeats the search against your existing reservation and prepares the replacement booking. Very short list since Pruvo left.

One hard requirement runs through all of it: you must have booked a refundable rate. If you took the lower-priced non-refundable rate, nothing on this page can help you, and anything that implies otherwise is misleading you.

The five options, ranked

1. Arbitrica

Closest thing to a like-for-like Pruvo replacement, and it adds an axis Pruvo never covered.

A personal AI agent that lives in your browser and works on two kinds of arbitrage. The first is multiple-time arbitrage: after you book a refundable hotel, Arbitrica's approach repeats the search at many points in time until the free-cancellation window ends, and when it finds a lower price for the identical hotel it prepares the rebooking, so you act on the difference rather than just being told about it. The new booking is confirmed before the original is cancelled. The second is geographic arbitrage, live at checkout: it searches the same hotel from 195 countries, finds the lowest price at a different seller from the one you are on, checks that it includes all taxes and fees and will not suddenly change, then prepares the whole booking so you only review and pay. Research showed that people earn on average $47 per hotel booking with Arbitrica, and up to $3,000 a year.

Where Pruvo moved to business-only, Arbitrica works for both sides: individual travelers, and companies, including teams traveling to trade shows and conferences, operations managers and travel managers.

Why it is first

  • It closes the loop. Alerting tools tell you the price fell; Arbitrica has the replacement booking prepared.
  • It is the only entry here that also asks whether the same hotel is lower-priced booked from another country, which is a second, independent source of savings on the same reservation.
  • The lower price can come from any seller, so a drop on a site you did not book through still counts.

Where it loses

  • Requires a refundable rate for the rebooking half, same as everything else in this category.
  • Short track record. Pruvo ran a consumer service for years and had the public receipts to prove it worked. We do not have that yet, and you should weight a new product accordingly.
  • Cancel-and-rebook has real friction that no agent can remove: a new card authorization, a lost confirmation number, and occasionally a hotel that had your original reservation flagged for an upgrade you now lose.

2. Google Hotels price tracking

Free, zero setup, and the correct first move for almost everyone. It will not rebook anything.

Since April 2026 Google tracks prices for individual properties, not just destinations. Open the hotel, set your dates, turn on Track prices, and Google emails you when the rate for those exact dates changes significantly. You can track several properties for the same trip. It currently requires you to be signed in and has rolled out in English and Spanish.

Strengths

  • Nothing to install, nothing to sign up for, and it already knows the hotel you were looking at.
  • Date-specific rather than vague. The alert is about your nights, which is the only version of this that is useful.
  • Works fine alongside anything else here, so there is no reason not to switch it on.

Limitations

  • It is an alert, not a rebooking. Every step after the email is manual, and the manual steps are where people give up.
  • It only sees partners who feed Google their rates, which leaves out a lot of local distribution.
  • Google does not know you have a booking. It is watching a search, not your reservation, so it cannot tell you whether the new price actually beats what you paid.

3. RatePunk

Strongest at the comparison side. Post-booking is a feature, not the point of the product.

A browser extension and app that compares the same hotel on Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, Agoda, Priceline and Airbnb while you browse, with price tracking and price drop notifications layered on.

Strengths

  • Broad seller coverage, so a drop on any one of six sites gets caught.
  • Combined review scores from several sites, which prevent more bad bookings than price tools save money.
  • Core features are free, with optional paid tiers.

Limitations

  • Alerts only. You rebook, you cancel, you chase the refund.
  • Paid tiers sit on top, and RatePunk does not publish a single clear price list, so the free version is a subset of what the marketing shows and the paid price is something to check at signup.
  • It operates its own booking site, so its recommendations are not neutral. Neither are ours. Worth knowing about both.

4. The manual method

Competitive if you take one or two trips a year. Useless if you take twelve.

Book the refundable rate. Put three calendar reminders in: one a month out, one two weeks out, one four days out. On each, search the same hotel again for the same dates. If it is lower, book the new rate, then cancel the old reservation, in that order and never the reverse.

  1. Book refundableNote the exact cancellation deadline and the hotel's time zone.
  2. Set three remindersA month out, two weeks out and four days out.
  3. Search the identical bookingSame hotel, dates, guests and rate conditions, compared on the final total with taxes and fees.
  4. Book first, cancel secondOnly cancel the original once the new confirmation is in your inbox.

Strengths

  • Costs nothing, installs nothing and shares nothing about your bookings with anybody.
  • You see the whole market rather than whichever sellers a tool has integrations with.
  • The four-days-out check catches the most common drop, which is a property discounting into a soft night.

Limitations

  • You will stop doing it. Everybody stops doing it. That is the entire commercial basis of this product category.
  • Three checks is a thin sample against a rate that moves daily, so you will miss most of the dips.
  • No practical way to check what the same hotel costs from other countries by hand beyond a couple of VPN attempts.

5. Hopper

Listed because people keep suggesting it for this, and it does not do this.

Hopper watches hotels and forecasts prices before you book, notifies you on drops and on private rates you qualify for, and sells Price Freeze, which locks a hotel rate for a fee for up to 60 days and covers the difference if the price rises, up to a capped amount.

Strengths

  • Price Freeze is a real and unusual product, and it is the right tool for a trip you are not yet sure about.
  • Access to private rates you would not otherwise be shown.
  • The best mobile experience of anything on this page.

Limitations

  • Tracking stops when you book, which is the exact window this page is about. For post-booking rebooking, it is the wrong product.
  • Price Freeze costs money whether or not you end up needing it, and the difference it covers is capped rather than unlimited.
  • Hopper states it does not offer price matching for hotel reservations.

Pruvo, for the record

Not an option for travelers any more. Still the best explanation of what this category is supposed to do.

Pruvo launched in 2016. You forwarded a refundable hotel confirmation to it, it monitored the rate around the clock, and it emailed you when you could rebook for less. It was free to travelers and made its money as an affiliate of the booking sites it sent the new reservation to. Web Travel Group announced its acquisition of Pruvo in 2026, and Pruvo has repositioned as a business platform selling dynamic pricing, hotel mapping and a partner marketplace to travel companies. Reported deal values differ between outlets and the transaction was described as subject to closing conditions, but the consumer side is unambiguous: bookings are no longer supported.

If you have an old reservation from the consumer service, Pruvo has pointed people at info@pruvo.com. If you find a 2026-dated list still recommending Pruvo to travelers, that is a reliable sign the list was not checked before publishing. Our earlier Arbitrica vs Pruvo article was written before Pruvo left the consumer market.

What we would actually do

Book refundable rather than lowest. The refundable rate usually costs a modest premium over the locked one, and that premium is what buys you the right to change your mind, which is the only reason any of this works.

Then switch on Google Hotels tracking, because it costs nothing and takes a moment, and add something that repeats the search against the reservation itself rather than watching a search. If you take one trip a year, the calendar reminders are fine and you can close this page. Frequently asked questions

Is Pruvo still available for travelers?

No. Following Web Travel Group's announced acquisition, Pruvo now operates as a business-to-business platform and consumer bookings are no longer supported. Lists that still recommend it to individual travelers are out of date. Arbitrica serves both individual travelers and companies, such as teams traveling to trade shows and conferences.

Do hotel prices really drop after you book?

Frequently enough that watching pays. Hotels run revenue management systems that reprice against a live occupancy forecast, so a night that is selling behind plan gets discounted as it approaches. The direction is not guaranteed on any given booking, which is why the refundable rate matters more than the timing of your booking.

Can I rebook a non-refundable hotel at a lower price?

No, and this is the single most common misunderstanding about the category. Everything here depends on your ability to cancel the original reservation without penalty. If you booked non-refundable, your options are asking the hotel directly for a date change rather than a refund, which managers approve far more readily, and checking whether the card you paid with carries trip cancellation cover.

Does cancelling and rebooking annoy the hotel?

Usually the hotel never notices, because both reservations sit inside the same channel. Where it can cost you is on a booking that had been flagged for an upgrade or a note on your loyalty profile, since the new reservation arrives clean. On a stay you care about, it is worth emailing the property after rebooking to re-attach anything that mattered.

Does rebooking work for anything other than hotels?

Yes. The same approach works on anything bought with a cancellation or return window: flights on flexible fares, car rentals, event tickets, pre-orders and delayed-shipment purchases on sites like Amazon, and subscriptions. Arbitrica's approach applies the same repeated search to those purchases, and the same rule holds: the original has to be cancellable without penalty.
