Best hotel rebooking tools in 2026

Pruvo was the default answer for watching a hotel rate after you book. It is now B2B only. Here is what actually replaces it.

Last updated 3 September 2026

For most of the last decade, the answer to 'what watches my hotel price after I book' was Pruvo. It was free, it worked, and every list pointed at it. Web Travel Group has announced its acquisition of Pruvo, and Pruvo now presents itself as a business-to-business platform. Consumer bookings are no longer supported. So the question is open again, and most of the lists you will find have not caught up.

Who wrote this. We make Arbitrica and we have put it first. Treat that accordingly. What we have done to make the page worth reading anyway: every tool gets its real limitations written out, ours included and at length; we have listed the free manual method as a genuine option, because for a lot of people it is the right answer; and we have kept the entry on Pruvo in place rather than quietly deleting a competitor, because knowing what happened to it is the most useful single fact on this page.

What rebooking actually means, and why most tools do not do it

Hotels reprice continuously. A property that is tracking behind its occupancy forecast for a Tuesday in February will discount into that Tuesday as it gets closer. If you are holding a refundable reservation, that price drop is money you can collect: you book the same hotel again at the lower rate and cancel the original. Nothing about that is a loophole. It is the flexibility you already paid for when you chose the refundable rate over the locked one.

The catch is that it only works if somebody is watching, every day, until check-in. Almost nobody does this by hand for more than one trip. So the category splits into three tiers:

  • Alerting. Tells you the price moved. You do the rest. Google Hotels, RatePunk.
  • Pre-booking only. Watches until you commit, then stops, which is the wrong half of the timeline. Hopper.
  • Actual rebooking. Monitors your existing reservation and prepares the replacement booking. Very short list since Pruvo left.

One hard requirement runs through all of it: you must have booked a refundable rate. If you took the cheaper non-refundable rate, no tool on this page can help you, and any tool that implies otherwise is misleading you.

1Arbitrica

Closest thing to a like-for-like Pruvo replacement, and it adds an axis Pruvo never covered.

A free Chrome extension that does two things. After you book a refundable hotel, it checks the rate several times a day and prepares a rebooking at the lower price when the rate drops, which you confirm in one click, so you capture the difference rather than just being told about it. Separately, at checkout, it searches the same hotel from 200 countries and finds the lowest price, then prepares the whole booking so you only review and pay. Arbitrica's own claim is up to $5,000 a year recovered for heavy travellers, which is a company figure and not something independently audited.

Why it is first

  • It closes the loop. Alerting tools tell you the price fell; this one has the replacement booking ready when you open the email.
  • It is the only tool here that also asks whether the same hotel is cheaper booked from another country, which is a second, independent source of savings on the same reservation.
  • Free, no card, no account tier to fall out of.

Where it loses

  • Chrome extension only. No mobile app, no Safari, no Firefox. If you booked on your phone in an airport, it was not there.
  • Requires a refundable rate for the rebooking half, same as everything else in this category.
  • Hotels only. Flights, cars, Amazon and event tickets are either announced or absent.
  • Short track record, and currently in early access rather than open general release. Pruvo ran a consumer service for years and had the public receipts to prove it worked. We do not have that yet, and you should weight a new product accordingly.
  • Free is currently framed as free for the first 10,000 users, which is a signal that the pricing may not stay this way.
  • Cancel-and-rebook has real friction the tooling cannot remove: a new card authorisation, a lost confirmation number, and occasionally a hotel that had your original reservation flagged for an upgrade you now lose.

2Google Hotels price tracking

Free, zero setup, and the correct first move for almost everyone. It will not rebook anything.

Since April 2026 Google tracks prices for individual properties, not just destinations. Open the hotel, set your dates, turn on Track prices, and Google emails you when the rate for those exact dates changes significantly. You can track several properties for the same trip. It currently requires you to be signed in and has rolled out in English and Spanish.

Strengths

  • Nothing to install, nothing to sign up for, and it already knows the hotel you were looking at.
  • Date-specific rather than vague. The alert is about your nights, which is the only version of this that is useful.
  • Works fine alongside anything else here, so there is no reason not to switch it on.

Limitations

  • It is an alert, not a rebooking. Every step after the email is manual, and the manual steps are where people give up.
  • It only sees partners who feed Google their rates, which leaves out a lot of regional and local distribution.
  • Google does not know you have a booking. It is watching a search, not your reservation, so it cannot tell you whether the new price actually beats what you paid.

3RatePunk

Strongest at the comparison side. Post-booking is a feature, not the point of the product.

A browser extension and app that compares the same hotel across Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, Agoda, Priceline and Airbnb while you browse, with price tracking and price drop notifications layered on.

Strengths

  • Broad seller coverage, so a drop on any one of six sites gets caught.
  • Combined review scores across sites, which quietly saves more bad bookings than price tools save money.
  • Free core product.

Limitations

  • Alerts only. You rebook, you cancel, you chase the refund.
  • Paid tiers sit on top, and RatePunk does not publish a single clear price list, so the free version is a subset of what the marketing shows and the paid price is something to check at signup.
  • It operates its own booking site, so its recommendations are not neutral. Neither are ours. Worth knowing about both.

4The manual method, free forever

Genuinely competitive if you take one or two trips a year. Useless if you take twelve.

Book the refundable rate. Put three calendar reminders in: one a month out, one a fortnight out, one four days out. On each, re-search the same hotel for the same dates. If it is cheaper, book the new rate, then cancel the old reservation, in that order and never the reverse.

Strengths

  • Costs nothing, installs nothing, shares nothing about your bookings with anybody.
  • You see the whole market rather than whichever sellers a tool has integrations with.
  • The four-days-out check catches the most common drop, which is a property discounting into a soft night.

Limitations

  • You will stop doing it. Everybody stops doing it. That is the entire commercial basis of this product category.
  • Three checks is a thin sample against a rate that moves daily, so you will miss most of the dips.
  • No practical way to check what the same hotel costs from other countries by hand beyond a couple of VPN attempts.

5Hopper

Listed because people keep suggesting it for this, and it does not do this.

Hopper watches hotels and forecasts prices before you book, notifies you on drops and on private rates you qualify for, and sells Price Freeze, which locks a hotel rate for a fee for up to 60 days and covers the difference if the price rises, up to a capped amount.

Strengths

  • Price Freeze is a real and unusual product, and it is the right tool for a trip you are not yet sure about.
  • Access to private rates you would not otherwise be shown.
  • The best mobile experience of anything on this page.

Limitations

  • Tracking stops when you book, which is the exact window this page is about. For post-booking rebooking, it is the wrong product.
  • Price Freeze costs money whether or not you end up needing it, and the difference it covers is capped rather than unlimited.
  • Hopper states it does not offer price matching for hotel reservations.

XPruvo, for the record

Not an option for travellers any more. Still the best explanation of what this category is supposed to do.

Pruvo launched in 2016. You forwarded a refundable hotel confirmation to it, it monitored the rate around the clock, and it emailed you when you could rebook cheaper. It was free to travellers and made its money as an affiliate of the booking sites it sent the new reservation to. Web Travel Group announced its acquisition of Pruvo in 2026, and Pruvo has repositioned as a business platform selling dynamic pricing, hotel and room-type mapping and a partner marketplace to travel companies. Reported deal values differ between outlets and the transaction was described as subject to closing conditions, but the consumer side is unambiguous: bookings are no longer supported.

If you have an old reservation from the consumer service, Pruvo has pointed people at info@pruvo.com. If you find a 2026-dated list still recommending Pruvo to travellers, that is a reliable sign the list was not checked before publishing.

What we would actually do

Book refundable rather than cheapest. The refundable rate usually costs a modest premium over the locked one, and that premium is what buys you the right to change your mind, which is the only reason any of this works.

Then switch on Google Hotels tracking, because it is free and takes ten seconds, and add one tool that watches the reservation itself rather than a search. If you take one trip a year, the calendar reminders are fine and you can close this page.

Frequently asked questions

Is Pruvo still available for travellers?

No. Following Web Travel Group's announced acquisition, Pruvo now operates as a business-to-business platform and consumer bookings are no longer supported. Lists that still recommend it to individual travellers are out of date.

Do hotel prices really drop after you book?

Frequently enough that watching pays. Hotels run revenue management systems that reprice against a live occupancy forecast, so a night that is selling behind plan gets discounted as it approaches. The direction is not guaranteed on any given booking, which is why the refundable rate matters more than the timing of your booking.

Can I rebook a non-refundable hotel at a lower price?

No, and this is the single most common misunderstanding about the category. Every tool here depends on your ability to cancel the original reservation without penalty. If you booked non-refundable, your options are asking the hotel directly for a date change rather than a refund, which managers approve far more readily, and checking whether the card you paid with carries trip cancellation cover.

Does cancelling and rebooking annoy the hotel?

Usually the hotel never notices, because both reservations sit inside the same channel. Where it can cost you is on a booking that had been flagged for an upgrade or a note on your loyalty profile, since the new reservation arrives clean. On a stay you care about, it is worth emailing the property after rebooking to re-attach anything that mattered.

Which of these is free?

Arbitrica, Google Hotels and the manual method are free. RatePunk has a free core with paid tiers. Hopper is free to use, but Price Freeze carries a fee. Pruvo is no longer available to consumers at any price.
