Key takeaways

  • Across 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, about 80% of search countries showed a lower price than the traveller's own country, with a typical gap of 20-40%.
  • In a second study of 1,500 hotels and about 1,000,000 searches from 100 countries, 84% of hotels had a lower price available from a different search country than a European or North American baseline.
  • Where a lower price existed, it averaged 18% below Booking, the market leader; 24% of hotels had a gap of 20% or more.
  • Searches from India, Vietnam and Thailand found the lowest prices (15% below the comparison point on average); searches from the UAE (+8%), Canada (+7%), Sweden and Denmark (+6%) paid the most.

The price a traveller sees for a hotel depends on more than the hotel and the dates. It also depends on the country they search from. Arbitrica searched the same hotels from 100 countries and found that a lower price from somewhere else is the norm, not the exception. This report puts the numbers in one place, with the tables, the definitions and a suggested citation.

Study 1: 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries

Arbitrica searched 1,000 hotels from 100 countries and measured each country's price against the price in the traveller's own country. About 80% of the search countries showed a lower price. The typical gap between the price shown and the lowest price found was 20-40%.

The headline is not that one country is always cheapest. It is that a lower price exists somewhere for most hotels, and the country that has it keeps changing.

Study 2: how big the gap is across 1,500 hotels

The second study ran about 1,000,000 searches for 1,500 hotels from 100 countries. 84% of hotels (1,275 of 1,500) had a lower price available when searched from a different country than a European or North American baseline. Where that was the case, the better price was on average 18% lower than the price on Booking.

Why the same hotel has different prices in different countries

Hotels and booking sites do not sell one price to the whole world. Different countries get access to different inventory, sales channels, local rates and promotions, so searching the same hotel from another country can open up options that are not offered in your own country. Currency, taxes shown at checkout and the rate type (refundable or not, breakfast or not) add to the gap, and all of it moves with dates and demand.

Checking this by hand means searching one country at a time with a VPN, which is slow and misses most of the options. Arbitrica, a personal AI agent, searches the same hotel from 195 countries, checks that the final total includes all taxes and fees, and prepares the booking at the lowest price in the world for the traveller to review and pay.

Method, definitions and limits

  • When: the searches for both studies were run between mid-2025 and mid-2026.
  • Study 1: 1,000 hotels, each searched from 100 countries. Measure: the share of search countries showing a lower price than the traveller's own country, and the typical gap between the price shown and the lowest price found.
  • Study 2: 1,500 hotels, about 1,000,000 searches from 100 countries. Measures: the share of hotels with a lower price from a different search country than a European or North American baseline; the gap distribution; the average gap to Booking where a lower price existed; and the average price position of each search country and region against the cheapest available price across the 100 search countries.
  • Hotel examples: individual price checks with the same hotel and dates, published with full data on the price-check page.
  • Limits: prices change with dates, demand and availability, so a single search can differ from these averages. The country and region figures are averages across many hotels, not a promise for any one booking.
