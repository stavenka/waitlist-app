Most travelers check a hotel price once, on one site, from their own country, and pay. A hotel price tracker exists because that single check misses most of what the same hotel can cost. Arbitrica is a personal AI agent that lives in your browser and shows up at the checkout when you are about to pay. It searches the same hotel from 195 countries and finds the lowest price on Earth for it at a different seller from the one you are on: Booking, Expedia or anyone else.

How the price tracker works

Different countries get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions. Searching the same hotel from another country can open up lower prices you cannot get from your own country, and the same hotel is often 5-30% cheaper when searched from somewhere else. Checking that by hand, with a VPN, one country at a time, is impossible in practice for 195 countries. Arbitrica does it for you.

In a test of 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, 80% of countries showed a lower price than the traveler's own country, and the typical gap was 20-40%. A tracker that only watches your own country cannot see that gap, however often it checks.

Two kinds of tracking: place and time

Hotel prices vary in two directions. They vary by place, because each country gets different rates. They also vary over time, because rates move as demand, cancellations and inventory change between the day you book and the day you check in. Arbitrica is built around both.

Live arbitrage at checkout

At the moment you are about to pay, Arbitrica runs the search from 195 countries in real time. If it finds a lower price for the identical hotel, it prepares the booking automatically so you only review and pay.

Multiple-time arbitrage after you book

A booking with free cancellation leaves a window in which a lower price is still useful to you. The mechanism Arbitrica developed repeats the search from 195 countries at many points in time after you book, until that window ends. When it finds a lower price for the same hotel, Arbitrica's approach prepares a rebooking at the lower price. The new booking is confirmed first, and only then is the original cancelled under its free cancellation policy.

What Arbitrica checks before it shows you a price

A lower number only helps if it is the same booking. Before Arbitrica shows you a price, it validates that:

  • it is for the identical hotel, with the same dates and the same room type you chose there;
  • the total includes all taxes and fees, so the figure is what you will actually pay;
  • the price is stable and will not suddenly change when you go to pay.

How to track a hotel price by hand

If you want to try it yourself first, this routine catches the most common drops:

  1. Book a refundable rateFree cancellation is what lets a later, lower price become money you earn. Note the deadline and the hotel's time zone.
  2. Record the full totalWrite down the final amount with taxes and fees, the rate name and what is included, such as breakfast.
  3. Set remindersCheck again a month out, two weeks out and a few days before the cancellation deadline.
  4. Compare like for likeSame hotel, dates, guests and rate conditions. A non-refundable rate is a different booking.
  5. Book first, then cancelOnly cancel the original once the new booking is confirmed.

This works for one trip. It gets hard with several trips, and it still only covers your own country. The guide to hotel price drops after booking covers the manual method in more detail.

When tracking pays off most

  • International hotels, where local distribution and country-specific promotions widen the gap between countries.
  • Bookings made weeks ahead, which leave a long window for the price to move.
  • Shoulder seasons, when hotels adjust rates often to fill nights.
  • Longer and more expensive stays, where the same percentage is a bigger amount.
  • Team travel to trade shows and conferences, where many bookings move together. Arbitrica also serves companies, including the operations managers and travel managers who book for them.
Tip

Tracking does nothing for a non-refundable rate after you pay. If a trip is more than a few weeks away, a flexible rate that costs a little more on day one often ends up the better deal.

Price alerts versus a tracker that acts

Many trackers stop at an alert. You get an email saying the rate dropped, and the rest is up to you: find the identical hotel at the new price, check that taxes and fees are included, read the cancellation terms, book again and cancel the original before the deadline. Plenty of people mean to do it and never do, and the lower price is lost.

Alerts also tend to watch one site from one country. If the lower price is on another site, or only available when the hotel is searched from another country, an alert from your own country never fires. Arbitrica's approach covers both gaps: the search runs from 195 countries, and when a lower price for the identical hotel appears, the new booking is prepared for you to review and pay, then confirmed before the original is cancelled.

Frequently asked questions

What is a hotel price tracker?

A hotel price tracker watches the price of a hotel for you and finds a lower price for the same hotel, at checkout or after you book. Arbitrica searches the same hotel from 195 countries and repeats the search until free cancellation ends.

Does hotel price tracking work on non-refundable bookings?

Only before you pay. After a non-refundable booking, a lower price cannot be used because cancelling would forfeit the original payment.

Will I be left without a hotel if a rebooking fails?

No. The new booking is confirmed first, and only then is the original cancelled. If the lower price is gone, the original booking stays as it is.

Does Arbitrica only track hotel prices?

No. The same mechanisms apply to online shopping such as Amazon, flights, car rentals, event tickets and subscriptions.
