## What comparison sites leave out

Hotel comparison sites and metasearch engines are useful for a first look. They have three blind spots worth knowing about:

- **One country.** Every price is searched from where you are. Rates distributed through local partners and promotions aimed at other countries never appear.

- **Partner lists.** They show the sellers that send them prices. Sellers outside that list are not in the comparison at all.

- **Headline prices.** Some results show a nightly rate before taxes and fees, so the lowest number on the page is not always the lowest total.

Rate parity agreements also mean the big booking sites often show the same price for the same hotel, so a comparison from one country can end in a tie. The gaps tend to open up between countries, not between sites.

## Why searching from other countries matters

The same hotel is often 5-30% cheaper when searched from another country. In a test of 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, 80% of countries showed a lower price than the traveler's own country, and the typical gap was 20-40%.

Independent research shows the same pattern. A 2024 NordVPN study found that travel bookings made through US websites came out up to 70% higher than through websites elsewhere (its largest gap was a car rental). One example from that study: a seven-night stay in a triple room at a Milan hotel for a family of three cost about $1,216 searched from the US and $1,012 searched from Italy.

## How to compare hotel prices properly

Whether you use a comparison site, check by hand or let an agent do it, the same rules decide whether a lower price is real:

- **Compare the final total.** Go to the last step before payment, with taxes, resort fees and service charges included.

- **Match the rate conditions.** Free cancellation, breakfast and pay-later terms change what you are buying.

- **Check the currency.** If a price is shown in another currency, the number that counts is what your card is charged, including any foreign transaction fee.

- **Look for eligibility rules.** Some rates are restricted to residents of a country or to members of a program.

- **Compare on the same day.** Hotel prices change daily, so a price from last week is not a fair comparison.

TipIf two booking sites show the same price, that is usually rate parity, not proof that you have found the lowest price. The next place to look is other countries.

## When a comparison site is enough, and when it is not

A comparison site does its job well when you book a domestic chain hotel in a market where rates are standardized. In that case the big booking sites often agree, the gap between countries can be small, and a quick look at a metasearch engine tells you most of what you need.

It falls short in a few common situations:

- **International trips**, where local partners and country-specific promotions make the gap between countries widest.

- **Independent and boutique hotels**, which often sell through a different mix of channels in each market.

- **Long or expensive stays**, where a gap of a few percent turns into a large amount.

- **Event dates**, when prices move fast and a comparison from this morning may already be out of date.

In those cases the comparison site gives you a starting price, not the lowest one. The lowest price is often found by searching the same hotel from countries you would not think to check.
