Best hotel price tools for international travel

Six tools ranked on one question: does it check the price of the same hotel from other countries, or only compare booking sites?

Last updated 3 September 2026

Most hotel price tools answer one question: which booking site is cheapest right now. That is a useful question, and it is also the question everyone already answers. This list ranks tools on a second one that almost nobody covers: does it check the price of the same hotel from other countries?

Who wrote this. We make Arbitrica, and we have ranked it first. That is a conflict of interest, so the standard we held ourselves to is this: every tool below gets its real limitations written out, including ours, and the ranking criterion is stated before the ranking so you can disagree with it. If you only care about which booking site is cheapest today, RatePunk is a better answer than we are, and it is at number two for that reason.

The criterion, stated first

Hotel prices vary along three independent axes. Most tools cover one.

  1. Seller. The same hotel on Booking.com, Expedia, Agoda and the hotel's own site. Gaps here are usually a few percent, occasionally more when a site is running a members-only rate.
  2. Time. The same hotel next Tuesday versus today. Rates drift after you book, sometimes downward.
  3. Country of search. The same hotel searched from another country. Different markets get different inventory, channels and local or regional rates, so searching from elsewhere can open up options that are not offered to you from your own country at all.

Axis three is the one people try to beat manually with a VPN, one country at a time, and mostly give up on. It is also the axis where the list of tools is very short. That is what this ranking is about, so a tool that is excellent at axis one and ignores axis three will rank below one that covers all three, even if the axis-one tool is more polished.

1Arbitrica

The only tool on this list that treats the country you search from as a price variable.

Arbitrica is a free Chrome extension. It does two separate things. At checkout it searches the same hotel from 200 countries, finds the lowest price, then prepares the whole booking so you review and pay. Separately, after you book a refundable rate, it checks the price several times a day and prepares a rebooking at the lower price when the rate drops, which you confirm in one click, so you capture the difference. Arbitrica's own claim is up to $5,000 a year recovered for heavy travellers, which is a company figure and not something independently audited.

What it does better than the rest

  • Covers all three price axes in one product. Everything else here covers one or two.
  • The country search is the genuinely uncontested part. Doing it by hand means VPN-hopping one country at a time, which realistically means checking three or four countries and missing the rest.
  • It prepares the booking rather than handing you a link and a research project.
  • Free, with no card required.

Where it loses

  • Chrome extension only. No mobile app, so it is no help mid-trip on a phone, which is exactly when a lot of hotels get booked.
  • Hotels only for now. Amazon purchases and event tickets are announced, not shipped.
  • The rebooking half needs a refundable rate. If you took the cheaper non-refundable rate, that feature does nothing for you.
  • Newest product here by a distance, and currently in early access rather than open general release. Google and Hopper have years of public track record and we do not.
  • Free is currently framed as free for the first 10,000 users, which is a signal that pricing may change. Read that how you want.
  • Not every hotel has a meaningful gap between countries. Chain properties in heavily standardised markets often price nearly flat, and on those the country search finds nothing and you have spent ten seconds for no gain.

Use it if you book internationally, in markets with a lot of local and regional distribution, and you book refundable. Skip it if you book domestic chain hotels non-refundable on your phone.

2RatePunk

The best answer to the question most people are actually asking: which site is cheapest right now.

A browser extension and app that runs a live comparison across major booking sites while you look at a hotel, including Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, Agoda, Priceline and Airbnb. It adds a combined review score built from several sites' ratings, plus price tracking and price drop alerts.

Strengths

  • Broad seller coverage, surfaced at the moment you are deciding rather than in a separate tab.
  • The aggregated review score is a real convenience, since the same hotel can be 8.1 on one site and 4.2 stars on another.
  • Core features are free.

Limitations

  • Compares sellers, not countries. If a rate is only distributed in Brazil, no amount of comparison from the UK will find it.
  • Optional paid tiers layer on top of the free product. RatePunk does not publish a full price list in one place, and the tier ranges quoted on third-party comparison pages do not match what its own site shows, so check the current price yourself before subscribing.
  • Its own booking site is part of the funnel, which is fine but worth knowing when it recommends itself.
  • Alerts tell you the price moved. Acting on it is still your job.

3Directo

Narrow job, done well: is the hotel's own site cheaper than the booking site you are on?

A free extension that compares the price in front of you against other platforms and the hotel's official site, side by side. It runs on Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera and Firefox, with an iOS Safari version in beta. The 300,000-plus user figure on its site is self-reported.

Strengths

  • Direct versus booking-site is a real gap that pure seller-comparison tools miss, and direct sometimes wins outright.
  • Booking direct usually means better treatment on changes, upgrades and loyalty nights, so the cheapest number is not always the whole comparison. Directo is the tool that puts that option in front of you.
  • Genuinely free, and light.

Limitations

  • No price history, no post-booking monitoring, no country dimension.
  • It has drifted beyond pure direct-versus-seller comparison and now also pushes its own negotiated deals, so it is no longer the neutral referee the original pitch implied.
  • Direct rates are frequently held at parity by contract, so on a lot of searches the answer is a tie and the extension has told you nothing new.

4Google Hotels

The free baseline everyone should be using, and the one with the least ambition.

In April 2026 Google extended price tracking from destination level to individual properties. Open a hotel, set your dates, switch on Track prices, and Google emails you when the rate for those dates changes significantly. Separately, and for much longer, Google Hotels has carried a Deal badge that flags properties priced below what its model expects for comparable hotels nearby on those dates.

Strengths

  • Free, no install, works on the device you already have, and it sits where you were going to search anyway.
  • Per-property tracking with email alerts is the single highest-value free feature in hotel pricing right now. Note it is currently limited to signed-in users and rolled out in English and Spanish.
  • Maps, reviews and photos in the same view, which matters more than most price tools admit.

Limitations

  • It shows you partners who feed Google their rates. Sellers outside that set are invisible, and that includes a lot of regional and local distribution.
  • No rebooking of any kind. It tells you the price changed and stops there.
  • No country-of-search dimension at all.
  • The Deal badge compares a hotel to its neighbours, not to what that same hotel costs elsewhere, which is a different and much weaker claim than it looks.

5Hopper

Good at the thing it does. That thing ends the moment you book.

Hopper watches a hotel, forecasts where the price is going, and pushes you a notification when it drops or when it can get you a private rate you are eligible for. Its Price Freeze feature locks a hotel rate for a fee, for up to 60 days, and covers the difference if the price rises before you commit, up to a capped amount rather than without limit.

Strengths

  • Price Freeze is a real product that nobody else on this list offers, and for a trip you are 80% sure about it is a rational purchase.
  • Access to private rates you would not see on a public search.
  • Strong mobile experience, which is the gap in most of the extensions here.

Limitations

  • Monitoring is pre-booking. Once you confirm, tracking stops, which is precisely the window where refundable rates drift down.
  • Price Freeze costs money and the protection is capped, so a large jump can outrun the cover. You are buying an option, and on trips where the price falls anyway you paid for nothing.
  • Hopper states it does not offer price matching for hotel reservations, so a cheaper rate found elsewhere is your problem.
  • Nothing on the country axis.

6Hotel Ninja

A lighter, simpler RatePunk. Fine, but there is little reason to choose it over the tools above.

A free extension that compares room rates across the major booking sites in real time, marketed at international travellers on the strength of multi-currency and multi-language support.

Strengths

  • Free, minimal, no account, no upsell path to speak of.
  • Currency handling is better than average for a tool this small.

Limitations

  • Multi-currency is not the same as multi-country. Converting the price you were already shown into another currency does not change which rates you are allowed to buy.
  • Very small: roughly 2,000 users on the Chrome Web Store, from what looks like a solo developer. That matters for how quickly seller coverage gets fixed when a booking site changes its markup, and for whether it is still maintained next year.
  • The multi-language claim is worth checking. The Chrome Web Store listing declares English only.
  • No tracking, no alerts, no post-booking anything.

What we would actually do

If you want one free thing: turn on Google Hotels price tracking for the property you are considering. It costs nothing and it catches the most common failure, which is booking three weeks before the rate softens.

If you want the money: book a refundable rate rather than the cheapest rate, then cover the seller axis with RatePunk or Directo at the moment of booking, and cover the country and time axes with something that runs on its own. Booking refundable typically costs a small premium over the locked rate, and that premium is the price of turning a purchase into an option you can re-decide later.

If you never book refundable and you never travel outside your own market, none of this is worth installing and Google Hotels alone is enough.

Frequently asked questions

Does a VPN get you cheaper hotel prices?

Not the way most articles describe it. The common claim is that a booking site quotes the same listing at a different price based on your IP address. That effect is small to nonexistent on the major booking sites. The real effect is different: different countries get access to different inventory, channels and local or regional rates, so searching the same hotel from another country can open up options that are simply not offered to you from your own country. A VPN can reach some of them, one country at a time, slowly. That is why tools exist for it.

Do hotel prices actually drop after you book?

Often enough to be worth watching. Hotels reprice continuously against occupancy forecasts, and a property that is tracking behind on a given night will discount into that night as it approaches. That is why the refundable rate matters more than the booking date. If you hold a refundable reservation, a price drop is money you can collect by rebooking and cancelling the original. If you hold a non-refundable rate, a price drop is just annoying.

Is booking direct with the hotel cheaper?

Sometimes, and the only way to know is to check both on the same day. Rate parity clauses mean the two are often identical. Where direct genuinely wins is on the things that are not the headline number: loyalty nights, upgrade priority, and a human who can change your dates without a third party in the middle.

Which of these is free?

Arbitrica, Directo, Google Hotels and Hotel Ninja are free. RatePunk has a free core with optional paid tiers. Hopper is free to use but Price Freeze carries a fee.

How we put this together

Tools were selected by whether a real person could install and use them today, which excluded several products that appear on other comparison lists but have shut down, gone business-only or never shipped a consumer version. Every factual claim about a competitor is sourced below and dated. Feature claims come from each product's own documentation and from independent coverage where it exists, not from our testing of competitors, and we have said so rather than implying a head-to-head test we did not run.
