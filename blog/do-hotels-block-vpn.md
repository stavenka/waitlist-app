# Do hotels block VPNs? Booking sites and hotel Wi-Fi explained

Updated 2026-11-10 by Arbitrica. Canonical: https://arbitrica.com/blog/do-hotels-block-vpn.html

## Key takeaways
- Some travel sites block known VPN addresses or show a captcha; FlyerTalk users report this on IHG, Expedia, Lufthansa and others, and it is inconsistent.
- Hotels can use VPN and proxy detection to stop "rate abuse", such as non-residents claiming resident-only rates. A card country that does not match the IP country is a common risk flag.
- Hotel Wi-Fi can block VPN connections by reserving ports or filtering traffic; switching protocol often helps.

## Short answer
Sometimes, in two different ways. Booking sites and hotel chains sometimes block VPN addresses or ask for a captcha, mainly to stop bots, and some hotels check location to protect resident-only rates. Separately, hotel Wi-Fi networks can block VPN connections. Neither is universal, and both are inconsistent from day to day.

## Do booking sites block VPNs?
Some do, some of the time. A FlyerTalk moderator wrote that VPN blocking happens occasionally and is not limited to IHG: some sites block certain VPN IP addresses because robots and spammers use them, Expedia and Blacklane show a captcha, and the same VPN can work one day and not the next ([FlyerTalk](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/29638412-post4.html)). Switching to another server and clearing cookies before connecting sometimes helps.

Booking, Agoda and Expedia do not publish a VPN ban in the sources we checked. Expedia told USA Today that a VPN does not change the price travellers see ([USA Today test, January 2026](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/articles/tried-using-vpn-score-cheaper-100134258.html)).

Hotels that offer resident or local-market rates can. ## Does hotel Wi-Fi block VPNs?
It can. Hotels limit VPN traffic to manage bandwidth and security, may reserve the ports VPN protocols use, and some firewalls detect encrypted VPN traffic ([VPNCentral](https://vpncentral.com/hotel-wifi-blocking-vpn)). The usual fixes are switching the VPN protocol to one that uses a different port, or using an obfuscated server, which is slower.

## How to avoid problems
- Read the rate conditions: resident-only rates say so, and may be checked at check-in.
- Use a card that can pay in that market, and expect checks when card and IP countries differ.
- Keep your booking details consistent: name, address and payment.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do hotels block VPNs?
Some do. Hotel and travel sites sometimes block VPN addresses or show captchas to stop bots, and hotels with resident-only rates may use VPN detection. It is inconsistent and depends on the site.

### Does Booking.com block VPNs?
Booking does not publish a VPN ban in the sources we checked. Some travel sites show captchas to VPN addresses, and card or residency checks can apply to certain rates.

### Can a hotel cancel a booking made with a VPN?
If the rate required residency or a local address and you do not meet it, the hotel can apply its rate conditions. Read them before you book.

### Why does hotel Wi-Fi block my VPN?
Hotels manage bandwidth and security, may close the ports VPNs use, and some firewalls detect VPN traffic. Switching protocol or using an obfuscated server usually helps.
