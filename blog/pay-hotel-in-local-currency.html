Should you pay for a hotel in local currency?

What dynamic currency conversion costs on a hotel bill, when paying in local currency wins, the booking-site version of the same choice, and the bigger saving most people miss.

Key takeaways

  • Paying in your home currency usually triggers dynamic currency conversion. Visa says the markup can be as much as 3-5%.
  • Paying in the local currency lets your card network convert at a more competitive rate; a card with no foreign transaction fee keeps it lowest.
  • On a $1,000 stay, 3-5% is $30-50. The gap between countries for the same hotel is often far larger: 20-40% in Arbitrica's research.

Short answer

Yes, in most cases pay for a hotel in the local currency. When you choose your home currency at the front desk or on a payment page, the hotel's payment provider converts it at its own rate, a practice called dynamic currency conversion. CardRatings, citing Visa, says this can add as much as 3-5% (CardRatings, citing Visa, August 2026). Paying in local currency lets your card network convert at a more competitive rate.

What dynamic currency conversion costs

CardRatings' examples: a 3% markup adds about $21 to a sample vacation day and over $140 across a week; on a 100-euro purchase the DCC rate cost about $1.92 more than the standard rate.

When to choose local or home currency

  • At the hotel: decline DCC when you give your card for the reservation and again at checkout (CardRatings, citing Visa, August 2026).
  • On booking sites: some show prices in your currency but charge in the hotel's, others convert for you. Compare the final amounts in one currency. Agoda reports of 3-5% currency markups are covered in Agoda VPN.
  • On Airbnb: paying in a currency other than the listing's adds to the guest fee (Airbnb Help Center).

The bigger saving most people miss

Choosing the right currency saves a few percent. Choosing the right market can save far more. Frequently asked questions

Is it better to pay a hotel in local currency?

Usually, yes. Paying in your home currency lets the hotel's processor convert at its own rate, which Visa says can add as much as 3-5%. Local currency lets your card network convert.

Is it cheaper to pay in local currency or home currency?

Local currency is usually less, especially with a card that has no foreign transaction fee. Dynamic currency conversion often costs more than a typical 3% card fee.

Should I pay Hotels.com or Booking in local currency?

Check the final total in both currencies before you pay; sites convert differently. At the hotel itself, decline dynamic currency conversion.

What is dynamic currency conversion at a hotel?

It is when the hotel's payment provider converts your bill into your home currency at its own rate, often with a markup and sometimes a separate fee.

What saves more than the currency choice?

Finding a lower price for the same hotel in another market. In Arbitrica's research the typical gap was 20-40%; Arbitrica searches 195 countries at the checkout.
