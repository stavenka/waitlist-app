Booking.com Different Prices by Country and VPNs, Explained

Why the same hotel can cost less when you search from another country, why a VPN only gets you part of the way, and what does the job properly.

Published June 2026 · Updated October 2026 · Arbitrica Team

Short version: Booking.com and other booking sites often show a lower price for the same hotel when you search from another country. Each country has access to different inventory, sales channels, local rates and promotions, so searching from somewhere else can open up prices you cannot get from your own country. A VPN lets you try one other country at a time, and checking all 195 countries by hand is impossible in practice. Why does Booking.com show different prices in different countries?

Short answer: A hotel does not sell through one single channel. It distributes its inventory through Booking.com, Expedia, wholesalers, local travel agencies and its own website, often under separate agreements for separate markets. Some rates and allotments are set aside for travelers in a given country, and many promotions only run in specific countries. Searching from another country opens up that country’s inventory, channels, local rates and promotions, and some of them are cheaper than anything available from your own country.

So the price you see from your own country is one slice of what exists for that hotel. The same hotel, on the same dates and with the same cancellation policy, can be cheaper when the search is made from a different country. In our experience the gap is often 5-30%.

A real example: Booking.com offered one hotel stay for $1,527.39. Arbitrica found the identical hotel and dates for $1,180.27, with all taxes and fees included. That earned the user $347.12, or 23%.

What changes when you search from another country?

Several things can be different for a search made from another country:

  • Inventory. Hotels give allotments to partners who sell mainly in one market, so some availability only shows up for searches from that country.
  • Channels. Local booking sites, travel agencies and wholesalers often sell in only a few countries.
  • Local rates. Some rates are negotiated for a specific market, such as domestic travelers.
  • Promotions. Seasonal sales, mobile deals and campaigns are often limited to one or a few countries.
  • How the total is shown. Some countries show prices with taxes and fees included, others add them at the end, so only the final total is a fair comparison.

The cheapest country changes from hotel to hotel and from day to day. There is no single “cheap country” you can always pick.

Can a VPN get you the cheaper Booking.com price?

Short answer: Sometimes, but slowly. A VPN lets you search from one other country at a time. You connect to a server, reload the hotel page, check the final total, and repeat for the next country. With 195 countries, doing that for every hotel you consider is not realistic.

The common problems with the VPN route:

  • One country per try. Each country takes a few minutes once you clear cookies, reload and get to the final total.
  • No way to know where to look. The cheapest country for one hotel is often not the cheapest for the next one.
  • Detection. Many websites recognize traffic from well-known commercial VPN servers, and the results you get may not match what a traveler in that country sees.
  • Totals that mislead. A lower headline price can end up higher once taxes, fees and currency conversion are added.
  • Rate conditions. Some local rates come with conditions such as residency. Read them before you book.

If you tried a VPN and saw no difference, that is common. The country you picked may not have a better rate for that hotel, or the VPN server may have been recognized. For a side-by-side view, see VPN vs. Arbitrica for cheaper hotel booking.

Is it legal to book a hotel at another country’s price?

Booking a published rate is an ordinary consumer purchase, and prices that differ by market are common in travel. What matters is the conditions of the specific rate: if a rate is restricted to residents of one country and you are not one, the hotel can refuse it. This is general information, not legal advice.

In Europe, regulators have looked closely at the contracts between Booking.com and hotels. In July 2024, Spain’s competition authority (CNMC) fined Booking.com about €413 million for abusing its dominant position in the Spanish hotel market. In September 2024, the Court of Justice of the EU ruled in Case C-264/23 (Booking.com v 25hours Hotel Company Berlin) that price parity clauses in contracts between Booking.com and hotels cannot simply be treated as ancillary restraints that fall outside EU competition rules.

What about booking site terms of service?

Booking site terms mostly target things like automated scraping for resale, payment fraud and abuse of cancellation policies. Common questions about Booking.com prices by country

Does this also happen on Expedia, Hotels.com and Agoda?

Yes. Prices for the same hotel differ by country on most large booking sites, not only Booking.com. Arbitrica searches the same hotel no matter who sells it, so the lowest price can come from Booking.com, Expedia or any other seller.

Will the hotel honor the cheaper rate when I arrive?

A booking at a lower rate is a normal reservation with a normal confirmation number. Read the rate conditions before you pay. If a rate is limited to residents of a specific country, the hotel can ask for proof at check-in.

What about chains like Marriott or Hilton?

Chain hotels also sell through many channels and run country-specific rates and promotions, so the same hotel can cost different amounts depending on the country you search from. Arbitrica looks for the lowest price for the identical hotel whoever sells it.

Can the hotel cancel a booking made at another country’s rate?

If you meet the conditions of the rate, it is a normal confirmed booking. The risk comes from rates with conditions you do not meet, such as residents-only rates, so check them before paying.
