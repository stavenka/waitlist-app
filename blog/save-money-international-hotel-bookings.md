International hotel bookings carry the highest price tags and the biggest potential for savings. A week in Tokyo, Paris or Dubai can run into thousands of dollars for accommodation alone. The tactics below lower that bill without downgrading your stay, and the biggest one is searching the same hotel from other countries.

## Book with free cancellation first

Choose a rate with free cancellation whenever the hotel offers one, even if the base rate is slightly higher. The flexibility to cancel and book again at a lower price usually outweighs the small premium. Treat your first booking as a price ceiling, not a final commitment.

## Use geographic price arbitrage

Different countries get access to different inventory, sales channels, local rates and promotions. Searching the same hotel from another country can therefore open up lower prices that you cannot get from your own country. The same hotel is often 5-30% cheaper when searched from somewhere else.

## Pay in the right currency

When a booking site or the hotel offers to charge you in your own currency, check the exchange rate it uses. A currency conversion offered at checkout often adds a markup over your card's own rate. Paying in the hotel's local currency with a card that has no foreign transaction fee is usually the lower-cost option. Whatever you choose, compare the final amount you will be charged, with all taxes and fees included.

## Know which taxes are paid at the hotel

Many destinations charge a city or tourist tax per person per night, and some hotels collect it at check-in rather than at booking. Resort fees and facility fees work the same way in some markets. When you compare two offers for the same hotel, check whether these charges are included in each total or listed as payable on arrival. An offer that looks lower because it leaves out a local tax is not lower.

## Check the rate conditions

Some local rates are limited to residents of a country or to holders of a local ID, and the hotel can ask for proof at check-in. Others are tied to a payment card or a membership. Read the conditions before you book and skip any rate you do not qualify for. Booking a rate that is openly offered in another market is a normal purchase.

## Search again after you book

Hotel prices keep changing between booking and check-in. If your reservation has free cancellation, a later drop is an opportunity: book the identical hotel again at the lower price, then cancel the original before the cancellation deadline. ## Don't rely on incognito mode

Searching in a private window is a popular tip. It clears cookies and your recent search history on that site, but it does not change the country you search from, so it cannot open up the prices available from other countries. Use it if you like, but do not expect it to replace the tactics above.

## Compare direct vs. OTA prices

Hotels sometimes offer lower rates on their own website to avoid paying OTA commissions. Check the hotel's site before completing an OTA booking. When the direct price is lower, you also get to deal directly with the property for any changes or issues. ## Consider alternative accommodation platforms

Beyond the major OTAs, platforms like Agoda (strong in Asia), Trip.com (competitive for Asian destinations) and local booking sites often carry their own rates. A hotel listed on five platforms can have noticeably different prices on each, especially for international destinations.

## Book further in advance for peak season

During high-demand periods, hotel prices tend to rise as the date approaches. For peak season, booking two to three months out usually gives you more choice and lower prices. The free cancellation strategy still applies: book early, keep searching, and book again if the price drops. The guide to the [best time to book hotels](https://arbitrica.com/blog/best-time-to-book-hotels.html) covers booking windows in more detail.

## Use loyalty programs strategically

Hotel loyalty programs often offer member rates slightly below the public rate, plus perks such as breakfast or late checkout. Check the member rate against the best price you can find from other countries, and take whichever is lower in total.

## A checklist before you pay

- **Same hotel, same terms**Confirm the hotel, dates, guests, room type, meal plan and cancellation policy match.

- **Full total**Include taxes, resort fees and any tax payable at the hotel.

- **Your currency**Compare the amount your card will be charged, after conversion and fees.

- **Free cancellation**Prefer a rate you can cancel, so you can rebook if the price drops.

- **Other countries**Search the same hotel from other countries, or let an agent do it from 195 at checkout.

## The same approach works beyond hotels

The same two mechanisms help with the rest of an international trip. Flights, car rentals and event tickets are also sold at different local prices in different countries, and a refundable flight or car rental can be booked again at a lower price before the cancellation deadline. The same applies to online shopping and subscriptions.

Often, yes. Different countries get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions, and the same hotel is often 5-30% cheaper when searched from somewhere else.

### Should I pay for a foreign hotel in my own currency or the local currency?
