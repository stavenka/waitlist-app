Is there a cheapest country to book hotels from?

Lists of "best countries" go out of date fast. The country with the lowest price changes with the hotel, the dates and the seller.

Search for the cheapest country to book hotels from and you will find confident lists: book from India, book from Thailand, book from Turkey. They are tidy, and they point at something real. The same hotel often has a different price in a different country. What does not exist is one country that wins every time.

This guide explains why the answer changes from hotel to hotel and from date to date, what actually makes a booking cheaper when it is made from another country, and how to find the lowest price without spending an evening switching VPN servers.

The short answer: there is no single cheapest country

The country that gives the lowest price for a hotel in Lisbon in May may not be the one that wins for the same hotel in October, and it may lose again for a hotel two streets away. Three things decide it:

  1. The hotel. Each hotel decides which markets get promotions, which agencies and wholesalers sell its stays, and which local rates it loads. A large chain and an independent guesthouse make very different choices.
  2. The dates. A promotion aimed at one country may run for two weeks and then stop. Inventory given to a partner in one market can sell out while the hotel still has availability elsewhere.
  3. The seller. The same hotel can be sold by Booking.com, Expedia, the hotel's own website, a local agency in another country and several wholesale partners. Which of them is active in a given country, and at what price, varies.

So the useful answer to "which country should I book from" is: it depends on the hotel, the dates and who sells it in that country, and you only find out by checking.

In a test of 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, 80% of countries showed a lower price than the traveler's own country, and the typical gap was 20-40%. Lower prices are common; the country that has them is what keeps changing.

Why the same hotel has a different price in a different country

When you search the same hotel from another country, you get access to a different set of offers. Different countries see different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions. A few mechanisms do most of the work.

Market-targeted rates and promotions

Hotels can create rates that are offered only to travelers booking from particular countries. A resort that wants more guests from a market it is trying to grow might run a discount there and nowhere else. These rates are real, bookable and honored at check-in. They come and go with the hotel's sales plans, which is one reason any list of "best countries" goes out of date quickly.

Different sellers in different countries

The big booking sites operate almost everywhere, but they are not the only sellers. In many countries, local online agencies, tour operators and bank or airline travel portals also sell hotel stays, often from inventory that wholesalers bought in bulk at negotiated rates. Searching from another country can put you in front of a seller you would never see from your own country, sometimes with a lower price for the identical hotel and dates.

Local currency and tax display

Some rates are set in a local currency and not adjusted every time the exchange rate moves, so a currency swing can make one country's price lower for a while. How taxes appear also differs. In the United States, hotel prices were long shown before taxes and resort fees; the Federal Trade Commission's fee rule, in force since May 2025, now requires mandatory fees to be included in the advertised price, though government taxes can still be listed separately. Much of Europe shows tax-inclusive totals. A price that looks lower may simply be missing something, so a fair check always compares the final total.

Member and app pricing

Member discounts, loyalty tiers and app-only rates are not rolled out the same way in every market. A logged-in discount you get in one country may not exist in another, and the reverse is also true. When you test prices, note whether you are logged in, because it changes what you are comparing.

Why "book from a cheaper country" lists mislead

Most lists rank countries by average income, on the theory that hotels charge less where people earn less. Sometimes that holds. Often it does not:

  • Promotions do not follow income. A hotel targeting a wealthy market with a launch discount can make that market the lowest for a month.
  • Some lower-income markets have fewer sellers, so the hotel's best offer may not appear there at all.
  • Some rates are restricted to residents, and the hotel can ask for proof at check-in. A rate you cannot use is not a saving.
  • Exchange rates move every day, which reshuffles the order without anyone changing a price.

The pattern you can rely on is narrower and more useful: the same hotel is often 5 to 30 percent cheaper when booked from somewhere else, and the somewhere else keeps changing. Independent research agrees that the gaps can be large: a 2024 NordVPN study found that travel bookings made through US websites came out up to 70% higher than through websites elsewhere (its largest gap was a car rental).

How to book a hotel from another country by hand

If you want to try it yourself, a careful process looks like this:

  1. Fix the hotel and dates firstChanging either one resets everything you have checked.
  2. Write down your baselineThe total price with taxes and fees, the cancellation terms and whether breakfast is included.
  3. Go private and switch countryOpen a private browser window, log out of booking accounts and connect a VPN to another country.
  4. Search the same staySame hotel, dates and guests. Record the total, not the headline price.
  5. RepeatTry as many countries as you have patience for, and check local agencies where you can read the site.
  6. Check conditions and fees before payingRead the rate conditions for residency rules, and check whether your card charges a foreign transaction fee. Many cards do, commonly around 3 percent, which can cancel out a small saving. Decline the option to pay in your own currency at checkout if the conversion rate is poor.
  7. Prefer refundable when the gap is smallThat way you can rebook if a lower price appears later.

This works, but it does not scale. Each country takes minutes, VPN connections are sometimes blocked, and even a patient traveler stops after five or ten countries. There are 195. The practical limits are covered in VPN vs. Arbitrica for hotel booking.

Frequently asked questions

Which country is cheapest to book hotels from?

No single country is always cheapest. The lowest price for the same hotel depends on the hotel, the dates and which sellers are active in each country, so the winning country changes from booking to booking.

Can I book a hotel from another country cheaper?

Often, yes. The same hotel is often 5 to 30 percent cheaper when booked from another country, because different countries get access to different inventory, sellers, local rates and promotions. Always compare the final total with taxes and fees.

Does a VPN alone make hotels cheaper?

Not by itself. Changing your connection on the same site rarely moves the price of the same rate much. Savings come from the different offers and sellers available in other countries, and finding them by hand means checking many countries one at a time.

Will the hotel honor a booking made from another country?

Yes, as long as the rate has no residency restriction. Read the rate conditions, because some local rates require proof of residence at check-in.
