## Weekly and monthly hotel rates

Many hotels, aparthotels and extended stay hotel brands offer lower nightly rates once a stay crosses a threshold, often at seven nights and again at around a month. These extended stay hotel discounts are not always shown by default. A few habits help you find them:

- **Test the threshold:** price six nights and seven nights, or 27 and 30. A longer stay can sometimes cost about the same as a shorter one if it unlocks a weekly or monthly rate.

- **Ask the hotel directly:** for stays of several weeks, the sales or reservations team may quote monthly hotel rates that are not loaded online.

- **Check what is included:** long stay rates can change housekeeping frequency, laundry, breakfast and parking. Price the stay as you will live it.

- **Look at aparthotels:** a kitchen and washing machine reduce daily costs on stays of several weeks.

Taxes can change with length too. The Texas Comptroller's hotel tax guidance, for example, treats a guest who has the right to stay for 30 consecutive days or more as a permanent resident exempt from the state hotel occupancy tax. Rules differ by country, state and city, so check local rules for your destination before you compare totals.

## Choosing the right hotel for a long stay

The lowest nightly rate is not always the lowest cost of living somewhere for a month. A standard hotel with no kitchen means eating out every day. A hotel far from where you work adds transport costs and time to every weekday. Before you compare prices, decide what the stay needs: a desk, a kitchen, laundry, parking, a quiet location or good transit links. Then compare only hotels that meet those needs, so the price you optimize is the price of a stay you would actually be happy with for weeks.

## Cancellation windows on long stays

Long stay bookings often come with stricter terms than short ones. Some hotels take a deposit, charge the first week up front, or make part of the stay non-refundable. Read three things before you book:

- **The cancellation deadline:** is it 24 hours before arrival, a week before, or never?

- **Early departure rules:** if you leave early, does the hotel recalculate the whole stay at the higher nightly rate?

- **Payment timing:** is the full amount charged now, weekly, or at checkout?

A flexible rate that costs a little more on day one is often the better deal on a long stay. It protects you if plans change, and it keeps the door open to rebooking at a lower price, which on a month-long stay can be worth far more than the difference between flexible and non-refundable.

TipFor long stays, ask whether the hotel will split the booking into separate weekly reservations. Each one can carry its own cancellation window, which gives you more chances to reprice later in the stay.

## Splitting a long stay into shorter bookings

One long booking is convenient, but it is not always the lowest total. Splitting a stay into two or more bookings can lower the price when demand changes during your dates, for example when an event or holiday falls in the middle of your month.

Splitting also has costs. Each split can mean a new check-in at the hotel. You may have to change rooms, or even hotels, and each booking can carry its own taxes and deposits. If you split at the same hotel, ask reservations to keep you in the same room so you do not have to pack up. Compare the combined total of the split bookings with the single long stay rate, including all taxes and fees, before you decide.

## Repricing a long stay over time

Hotel prices move every day with demand, occupancy and competitor rates. A long booking made weeks in advance spans many of those changes, which means many chances for the price of the same stay to fall below what you paid.

The method is straightforward. Book a refundable long stay, then keep checking the price until the cancellation deadline. If the identical hotel, dates and terms become cheaper, book again at the lower price and cancel the original only after the new booking is confirmed. For split bookings, repeat this for each part.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do hotels offer monthly rates?

Many do, especially aparthotels and extended stay brands. Rates are not always shown online, so for stays of several weeks it is worth asking the hotel directly and testing prices around the seven-night and one-month marks.

### Is it cheaper to split a long hotel stay into shorter bookings?

Sometimes, for example when an event raises prices in part of your dates. Compare the combined total of the split bookings, including all taxes and fees, with one long booking before deciding.

### Can I rebook a long stay if the price drops?

Yes, if the booking has free cancellation. Book the identical stay at the lower price, confirm the new booking, then cancel the original before the deadline.
