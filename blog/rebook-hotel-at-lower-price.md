Hotel prices often drop after you book. Here is how to cancel and rebook safely, and the pitfalls that can wipe out the saving.

Yes, you can get a lower price after booking a hotel. If your booking has free cancellation and the price of the same hotel drops before the deadline, you can book the new rate and cancel the old one. The saving is real, but so are the traps: a cancelled booking you cannot get back, a deposit that takes weeks to refund, or a lower rate that turns out to be for a different room. This guide walks through the process step by step so you rebook safely.

## Why hotel prices drop after you book

Hotel rates are set by revenue management systems that react to occupancy, demand and competitor prices every day. If a hotel is filling more slowly than expected, a conference is cancelled or a nearby hotel cuts its rates, the price of your stay can fall weeks after you paid. Our article on [why hotel prices drop after booking](https://arbitrica.com/blog/hotel-price-drop-after-booking.html) explains the causes in detail. This one is about what to do when it happens.

The key condition is flexibility. A price drop only helps you if your current booking can still be cancelled without a penalty. Everything below assumes it can.

### How often to check the price of your booking

There is no fixed schedule that guarantees you catch a drop, because hotel prices can change several times a day. A practical rhythm is to check every few days while the trip is far away, more often in the last two weeks before the cancellation deadline, and once more the day before it. Prices tend to move most in shoulder seasons and in the weeks around events, when hotels adjust rates to fill rooms.

Each time, search the same hotel, dates and room type, and note the total you would pay. A simple note on your phone with the date and the total makes it easy to see whether the price is trending down or up.

## Step 1: Check your cancellation terms before anything else

Open your confirmation and find three things:

- **The exact deadline.** "Free cancellation until 6 p.m. on the day before arrival" usually means 6 p.m. in the hotel's local time, not yours. Convert it.

- **What you get back.** Some rates refund in full, some keep a first-night charge, and some refund to a credit or voucher instead of your card.

- **Whether you paid already.** A prepaid booking means you are waiting for a refund. A pay-at-hotel booking may only involve a card hold, which is released rather than refunded.

If the booking is non-refundable, stop here. Cancelling it would cost you the full amount, and a lower price elsewhere does not change that. Your only route is a price match or guarantee, if the site you booked with still offers one and your claim meets its rules.

## Step 2: Confirm the new offer is really the same stay

A lower price is only a saving if it buys you the same thing. Check, side by side:

| Check | What to look for |

| Hotel | Same property, not a sister hotel with a similar name. |

| Dates and guests | Same check-in, check-out and number of adults and children. |

| Room type | Same bed configuration, view and size category. |

| Board | Breakfast included or not, in both offers. |

| Cancellation | The new rate should also be refundable if your trip is still weeks away. |

| Total | Final amount with all taxes and fees, including anything payable at the hotel. |

The last row matters most. A nightly rate that looks a little lower can end up higher once a resort fee or city tax is added. Go through to the final payment screen on the new offer before you decide.

## Step 3: Work out whether the saving is worth it

Subtract the new total from the old total, then take off anything the switch costs you: a currency conversion on the refund, a lost member benefit, or points you would no longer earn. If what remains is a few dollars, keep your original booking. If it is a meaningful amount, move on to the next step.

Do not trade away flexibility for a small saving. Swapping a refundable booking for a non-refundable one at a slightly lower price removes your chance to rebook again if the price keeps falling.

## Step 4: Book the new rate first, then cancel the old one

This order is the whole point. If you cancel first and the lower rate disappears while you are entering card details, or the hotel sells its last room of that type, you are left with no booking and a higher price.

- **Make the new booking**Complete payment or the card guarantee for the lower rate.

- **Wait for the confirmation**Check that the confirmation email shows the right hotel, dates, room type and total.

- **Cancel the original**Use the site or app where you made it, before its deadline.

- **Save the cancellation confirmation**Keep the email or screenshot with the cancellation number.

- **Check the refund**Watch your card statement until the refund or released hold appears.

## Pitfalls to watch for when you cancel and rebook

### Non-refundable and partially refundable rates

Some rates labeled "flexible" still charge the first night if you cancel within a set window. Read the cancellation policy line by line, not just the badge on the search results.

### Prepaid deposits and refund timing

If you prepaid, you will be out of pocket for both bookings until the refund lands, which can take several business days or longer depending on the site and your bank. Make sure your card limit can carry both amounts for a while.

### Pay-at-hotel holds

A pay-at-hotel booking often places a hold on your card rather than a charge. When you cancel, the hold should be released, but release times vary by bank. Keep the cancellation confirmation in case the hotel charges a no-show fee by mistake.

### Loyalty points, elite nights and member perks

If your original booking was made through a hotel loyalty program, moving to another site may mean losing points, elite night credit or perks such as late checkout. Put a rough value on those before you switch.

### Credits, vouchers and special requests

If you paid partly with a gift card, a travel credit or a voucher, a cancellation may return that portion as credit rather than cash. Special requests such as a crib, an accessible room or an early arrival note do not move with the booking; send them again for the new reservation.

## Can you ask the hotel or the site to match the lower price?

Sometimes. Some booking sites and hotel chains publish a price match or best rate guarantee, each with its own conditions about what counts as the same offer and when you can claim. Booking.com still has one, explained in our [Booking.com price match](https://arbitrica.com/blog/booking-com-price-match.html) guide. ## A short rebooking checklist

- Your current booking is refundable and the deadline is in your calendar, in your own time zone.

- The new offer is the same hotel, dates, guests, room type and board.

- You compared totals with all taxes and fees, not nightly rates.

- The new rate keeps free cancellation if the trip is still weeks away.

- The new booking is confirmed before you cancel the original.

- You saved the cancellation confirmation and checked the refund arrived.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you get a lower price after booking a hotel?

Yes, if your booking has free cancellation. Book the identical hotel at the lower price, wait for the confirmation, then cancel the original before its deadline.

### Should I cancel first or book first when rebooking a hotel?

Book first. If you cancel first, the lower rate or the last room of that type can disappear before you finish the new booking.

### What if my hotel booking is non-refundable?

Cancelling would cost you the full amount, so rebooking does not work. Check whether the site you booked with offers a price match and whether your booking meets its rules.

### How long does a hotel refund take after cancelling?

It depends on the site, the hotel and your bank. Prepaid refunds can take several business days or longer, and pay-at-hotel card holds are released on the bank's schedule.
