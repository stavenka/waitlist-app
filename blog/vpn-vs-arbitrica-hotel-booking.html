Savvy travelers have used VPNs for years to find cheaper hotel prices from other countries. The idea is simple: search the same hotel from another country and book there if the total is lower. It can work, but one country at a time is a slow way to look, and that is the problem Arbitrica was designed to solve.

Why searching from another country can lower a hotel price

Hotels do not sell through one channel. They distribute rates through online travel agencies, wholesalers, local partners, tour operators and their own websites, and each country gets its own mix of those channels. Different countries get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions, so searching the same hotel from another country can open up prices you cannot get from your own country.

Independent research points the same way. A 2024 NordVPN study found that travel bookings made through US websites came out up to 70% higher than through websites elsewhere (its largest gap was a car rental). In one example from that study, a seven-night stay in a triple room at a Milan hotel for a family of three cost about $1,216 searched from the US and $1,012 searched from Italy.

In a test of 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, 80% of countries showed a lower price than the traveler's own country, and the typical gap was 20-40%. The catch is that the cheapest country changes from hotel to hotel and from date to date, so there is no single country you can always switch to.

How the VPN approach works

You subscribe to a VPN service, connect to a server in another country (say, Thailand or Poland), and open the hotel on a booking site. If the final total is lower, you book. If not, you connect to another country and check again.

Done carefully, each check means clearing cookies or opening a private window, entering the same dates and guests again, getting all the way to the final price with taxes and fees, and noting the rate conditions. Then you switch servers and repeat.

VPN pros

  • Low cost: most VPN services charge a few dollars a month
  • Useful beyond hotel booking (streaming, privacy)
  • You choose which country to test
  • Available on most devices

VPN cons

  • One country at a time: checking 10 countries can take 30 minutes or more
  • No way to know which country has the best price without checking them all
  • Many websites recognize traffic from well-known VPN servers
  • Slower connections make each search take longer
  • You have to compare final totals, taxes, fees and rate conditions yourself
  • Nothing happens after you book, even if the price drops later
  • Every hotel, every trip, starts from zero

The math on finding the best price

If each country takes about three minutes to check properly, 10 countries take about 30 minutes and cover around 5% of the 195 countries. All 195 would take close to ten hours for a single hotel, and the answer changes from hotel to hotel and from day to day. Checking every country by hand is not feasible for any booking.

Arbitrica checks the same hotel from 195 countries every time. The same hotel is often 5-30% cheaper from the right country, and research showed that people earn on average $47 per hotel booking with Arbitrica, and up to $3,000 a year.

A real example: Booking.com offered one hotel stay for $1,527.39. Arbitrica found the identical hotel and dates for $1,180.27, with all taxes and fees included. That earned the user $347.12, or 23%.

If you do use a VPN, check these things

If you want to try the manual route for a trip, a few habits stop a lower number from turning into a worse booking:

  1. Compare the final totalGo all the way to the last step before payment. Some sites show prices before tax in one country and with tax in another.
  2. Match the rate conditionsA lower price on a non-refundable rate is not the same booking as a flexible rate with free cancellation. Check breakfast, cancellation and payment terms line by line.
  3. Watch the currencyA price shown in another currency is only lower if the amount charged to your card, including any foreign transaction fee, is lower.
  4. Read the residency termsSome rates are labeled for residents of a specific country and can ask for proof at check-in. Skip those unless they apply to you.
  5. Keep the confirmationSave the confirmation email and the cancellation terms, in case you need to change or cancel later.
Tip

Start with the countries you are least likely to guess. The lowest price often comes from an unexpected country, because promotions, channels and exchange rates keep changing.

When to use a VPN instead

VPNs remain useful for general online privacy and for reaching geo-restricted content. For the specific job of finding the lowest price for a hotel, Arbitrica is purpose-built: it covers 195 countries instead of one, does the checks for you and prepares the booking.

The same applies beyond hotels. Online shopping, event tickets, flights and car rentals also vary by country, and a VPN has the same one-country-at-a-time limit there.
