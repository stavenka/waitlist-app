Time Arbitrage Explained: How to Benefit When Prices Drop After You Buy

October 20, 2026 · Arbitrica Team

You book a hotel, order a pre-release console or buy concert tickets, and a week later the same thing is cheaper. Most people shrug and move on. But a lot of purchases come with a window in which you can still cancel, return or change them, and inside that window a price drop after purchase is money you can still get.

Time arbitrage is the name for doing that on purpose. This guide explains what it is, where it works, where it does not, and how it combines with geographic arbitrage.

What is time arbitrage?

Time arbitrage means getting the same item at a lower price by searching for it at more than one point in time. It works because prices for hotels, flights, tickets and many products change constantly, and because many purchases can be undone for a period after you pay.

There are two forms:

  • Live arbitrage happens at checkout, in real time. Right before you pay, the same item is searched again so you buy at the lowest price that exists at that moment.
  • Multiple-time arbitrage happens after you buy. The search is repeated at many points in time until the cancellation or return window ends. If the identical item becomes cheaper, a new purchase is prepared at the lower price, and the original is cancelled or returned only once the new one is confirmed.

Where price drops after purchase happen

Hotels

Hotels are the clearest case. Prices are set night by night and move with demand, competitor prices and cancellations, as we explain in why hotel prices change daily. Many rates come with free cancellation until a day or a few days before arrival. If the same hotel, dates and rate type get cheaper before that deadline, you can book the new price and cancel the old one. Our article on hotel price drops after booking goes through it step by step.

Flights

Airfares change as seats in each fare class sell. In the United States, Department of Transportation rules require airlines to let you either hold a reservation or cancel it without penalty within 24 hours of booking, when you book at least seven days before departure. Beyond that window, it depends on the ticket. Flexible fares can often be changed, and some airlines give credit for the difference when the fare drops. Basic fares usually allow very little.

Car rentals

Pay-later car rental reservations can usually be cancelled without a fee, and rental prices move a lot with demand around holidays and events. That makes them a good fit for repeated searches until pickup.

Event tickets

Ticket prices are increasingly dynamic. The 2024 Oasis reunion sale in the UK drew wide complaints when prices rose during the queue, and the Competition and Markets Authority opened an investigation into it. Prices can also fall, especially on resale platforms as an event gets closer and sellers compete. Time arbitrage only helps here when the purchase can be refunded, exchanged or resold, so the terms of the specific ticket matter more than for hotels. Read more on our event tickets page.

Pre-orders and delayed-shipment e-commerce

Online orders often have a gap between purchase and delivery, and prices keep moving in that gap. Amazon.com runs a pre-order price guarantee on eligible items: if its price falls between your order and the end of the release day, you pay the lowest price. For orders that are not covered, you can often cancel before the item ships and reorder at the new price, or use the return window after delivery. In the European Union, most online purchases of goods come with a 14-day right of withdrawal, which creates a similar window. Our Amazon page covers this side of things.

Subscriptions

Annual plans for software, streaming and other services change price with promotions, and they often cost different amounts in different countries. When a service lets you cancel and get a refund for a short period after an annual purchase, a lower price for the same plan during that period can be used in the same way.

How time arbitrage combines with geographic arbitrage

Time arbitrage asks when the price is lowest. Geographic arbitrage asks where. The same hotel or product can have a different price when bought from another country, because different countries get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions. Put the two together and every search becomes a grid: many countries at many points in time. A single search from your own country checks one cell of that grid. The lowest price is often in a cell nobody would think to check by hand, such as an unexpected country three days after you booked.

What to watch before you rebook or repurchase

Time arbitrage only pays when the new deal is truly the same and truly cheaper. Check these before you act:

  • Identical item. Same hotel, dates, guests, rate type and meal plan. For products, same model, condition and seller warranty.
  • Final total. Taxes, resort fees, booking fees, shipping and currency conversion all count.
  • Cancellation or return costs. Cancellation fees, restocking fees and return shipping can wipe out a small saving.
  • Deadlines. Hotel cancellation deadlines are often set in the hotel's local time. Return windows usually start on delivery, not purchase.
  • Order of steps. Secure the new booking first, then cancel the original, so you are never left without one.
  • Refund timing. Your card may show both charges for a few days until the refund clears.

Can you do time arbitrage by hand?

Yes, within limits. Book flexible rates, put the cancellation deadline in your calendar and check the same item every few days. Price history sites such as camelcamelcamel help for Amazon products, and airline and booking sites offer price alerts. The limit is effort: each check covers one moment from one country, and most people stop after a couple of tries. That is the gap an automated approach fills.

Frequently asked questions

What is time arbitrage?

Time arbitrage means getting the same item at a lower price by searching for it at more than one point in time, either at checkout (live arbitrage) or repeatedly after purchase while it can still be cancelled or returned (multiple-time arbitrage).

What can I do if the price drops after purchase?

If your purchase can be cancelled or returned, buy the identical item at the lower price first, then cancel or return the original before the deadline. Check fees and the final total so the saving is real.

Which purchases work best for time arbitrage?

Hotels with free cancellation, pay-later car rentals, flexible flights, pre-orders and online orders with a return window. Event tickets and subscriptions work only when the specific terms allow a refund, exchange or resale.

How does time arbitrage relate to geographic arbitrage?

Geographic arbitrage finds the lowest price for the same item from different countries. Time arbitrage repeats that search at different points in time. Combined, they check many countries at many moments.
