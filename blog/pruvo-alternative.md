## What happened to Pruvo
Pruvo was founded in 2016 to help travellers get a lower hotel price after booking: you forwarded your reservation, it watched for a lower rate, and you rebooked. In 2026 Web Travel Group, which owns the B2B accommodation marketplace WebBeds, agreed to buy Pruvo for about A$26.7 million (US$17.4 million) ([AltexSoft, June 4, 2026](https://www.altexsoft.com/travel-industry-news/web-travel-pays17-4-millon-for-pruvo-as-hotel-ai-gets-practical/)). Its CEO said Pruvo would continue as a standalone company within the group ([PhocusWire](https://phocuswire.com/web-travel-group-acquires-hotel-rebooking-platform-pruvo)), and Pruvo's site now carries the line "a WebBeds Group company".

Pruvo's homepage now says the company is "now exclusively a B2B platform" and that consumer bookings are no longer supported, as PhocusWire reported. Its product today is price monitoring and automatic rebooking for travel sellers, to improve their margins.

## Why the country you search from matters more than a price drop
A post-booking watch only sees one price moving over time, in your own market. The bigger gap is often between countries. Research across 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries found a lower price than the traveller's own in about 80% of countries, with a typical gap of 20-40%. In [Arbitrica's own price check](https://arbitrica.com/blog/hotel-prices-by-country-data.html), the same Istanbul hotel and 4 nights were $1,066 from the UK and $708 from India.

That is why Arbitrica starts at the checkout, before you pay, and searches the same hotel from 195 countries. Its approach then repeats the search after you book, and a new booking is confirmed before the original one is cancelled.
