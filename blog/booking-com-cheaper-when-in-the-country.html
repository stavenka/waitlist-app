Are hotel prices on Booking.com cheaper when you are in the country?

Not as a rule. In our checks the hotel's own country was never the single lowest location. Why, what changes in Peru, and how to find the lowest price for the same hotel.

Short answer

Not as a rule. Booking can show a different price depending on the traveller's country, because hotels can set rates per country, but the hotel's own country is not reliably the lowest. In Arbitrica's price checks it was never the single lowest location, and sometimes it was the highest. Taxes can also work against locals: in Peru, residents pay 18% IGV on hotel rates that foreign tourists can be exempt from.

Why your country changes the price on Booking

  • Country rates. Booking's developer documentation lists geo or country rates as "rates available based on the traveller's country" (checked on November 3, 2026). A hotel can offer one market a lower rate and leave another at the standard price.
  • Dynamic pricing. Prices also move with demand, dates and the seller's algorithms, so the same hotel can change between two searches an hour apart.
  • Different sites, different rates. Prices and available rooms differ by site as well as by country, so the lowest total may be on another booking site.
  • Taxes and how they are shown. Some countries tax residents and visitors differently, and a site may show the total with or without local taxes.

Are hotel prices in Peru on Booking.com cheaper when you are in the country?

Usually not for locals, and being physically in Peru does not lower the price for a visitor. Hotel rates in Peru are subject to 18% IGV, the general sales tax. Foreign or non-resident guests who stay less than 60 days can be exempt if they show the required document at check-in, as hotels state on their own rate pages (for example Dazzler Lima San Isidro by Wyndham, checked on November 3, 2026). SUNAT, Peru's tax authority, treats accommodation for non-domiciled guests as an export of services (report 175-2013).

So a Peruvian resident booking a hotel in Peru usually pays the 18% that a foreign tourist does not. For a visitor, what matters is your passport and residency at check-in, not where you search from. Check whether the total on Booking includes IGV, and keep your passport and entry record ready at the desk.

Resident and local rates

Some hotels publish resident rates for people who live in the country. They usually require local ID or an address at check-in, so they are only a saving if you qualify. Even hotel chains treat them as a separate category: LoyaltyLobby reported in May 2025 that Marriott's Best Rate Guarantee excludes rates that need a specific residency, ID or credit card. For a traveller, the useful question is not "where am I?" but "which country's price for this hotel can I book?"

How to check before you book

  1. Note the final total for your room, with taxes and fees, from where you are now.
  2. Check the same room, dates and cancellation policy from several other countries and on more than one site.
  3. Read what the total includes: local taxes, resident-only conditions and the currency.
  4. Book a rate with free cancellation and keep checking until the deadline, because prices move after you book.
