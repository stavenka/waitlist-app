Why the same hotel, product, seat or subscription can cost less when you search for it from another country, and how time arbitrage adds a second way to pay less.

Geographic price arbitrage means buying the same item at a lower price that is available from another country. The item does not change: it is the same hotel, the same product, the same seat or the same subscription. What changes is the country you search from, and with it the inventory, sales channels, local rates and promotions you can reach.

It works for far more than hotels. Online shopping, event tickets, flights, car rentals and subscriptions all have prices that vary by country. This guide explains why, shows how it plays out in each category, and covers its partner mechanism, time arbitrage.

## How geographic price arbitrage works

Sellers rarely offer one price to the whole world. A product or service is distributed through different channels in different countries: local retailers, wholesalers, local partners, marketplaces and the seller's own storefronts. Each country gets its own mix of those channels, along with local rates and promotions aimed at that market.

Searching the same item from another country can therefore open up lower prices that you cannot get from your own country. These prices are legitimate and can be booked or bought. Geographic arbitrage is the work of finding them. In practice it has four steps:

- **Pin down the exact item**The same hotel and dates, the same product model, the same seat category or the same plan.

- **Search from as many countries as possible**The lowest price rarely comes from the country you would guess first.

- **Confirm the lower price is real**Check that each lower price is for the identical item and includes all taxes, fees and, for physical goods, shipping and import costs.

- **Buy at the lowest total**Compare the final amounts, not the headline prices.

## Why prices differ by country

- **Purchasing power and local demand.** A price that sells well in one country may be too high to sell in another.

- **Local competition.** Markets with many alternatives tend to see lower prices.

- **Channels and contracts.** Different distributors and partners serve different countries, each on its own terms.

- **Promotions.** Campaigns are often aimed at a single country's market.

- **Currency and taxes.** Exchange rates move, and local taxes are built into prices differently.

Together these factors mean the same item can have many different prices at the same moment, depending on which country you search from. The gaps are not small. In a test of 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, 80% of countries showed a lower price than the traveler's own country, and the typical gap was 20-40%. A 2024 NordVPN study found that travel bookings made through US websites came out up to 70% higher than through websites elsewhere (its largest gap was a car rental).

## Where geographic arbitrage applies

### Hotels

Hotels sell through online travel agencies, wholesalers, local partners and their own sites, and each country sees a different mix of rates and promotions. The same hotel on the same night is often 5-30% cheaper when searched from another country. In one real Arbitrica search, Booking offered a hotel for $1,527.39. Arbitrica found the identical hotel for $1,180.27, earning the user $347.12 (23%). ### Online shopping and retail

Large retailers and marketplaces such as Amazon run separate storefronts for different countries, each with its own sellers, stock, promotions and prices. The same product can cost less on another country's storefront or from a seller in another country, even after shipping and import costs. The total, with those costs included, is what counts. See [how to buy online for less from another country](https://arbitrica.com/blog/buy-online-cheaper-from-another-country.html).

### Event tickets

Tickets for concerts, sports and festivals are often sold through different ticketing partners in different countries, with their own presales and promotions. The same seat category can be listed at different prices depending on where you buy it.

### Flights and car rentals

Airlines and rental companies sell through many points of sale, and the same flight or car can be priced differently when bought from another country's site or in another currency. Fare rules, taxes and insurance terms still apply, so the comparison has to be for the identical ticket or rental.

### Subscriptions and digital services

Streaming services, software and other subscriptions often set a separate local price for each country. Check the provider's terms first, since some limit where a country's plan can be used.

## Time arbitrage: the second mechanism

Geographic arbitrage looks at countries. Time arbitrage looks at moments. The price of the same item changes over time, so repeating the search at many points in time can find a lower price than the one you first saw. There are two forms.

### Live arbitrage

Live arbitrage happens at checkout, in real time. Just before you pay, the same item is searched from many countries at once, so you buy at the lowest price available at that moment rather than the price your own country happens to offer.

### Multiple-time arbitrage

Multiple-time arbitrage continues after you buy. As long as the purchase can still be cancelled or returned, the search is repeated. If the identical item becomes cheaper, a new booking or purchase is prepared at the lower price, and the original is cancelled only once the new one is confirmed. It applies to anything with a cancellation or return window: hotels with free cancellation, refundable flights, car rentals, refundable event tickets, pre-orders and delayed-shipment orders, and subscriptions that can be cancelled. The [time arbitrage guide](https://arbitrica.com/blog/time-arbitrage-explained.html) covers this in more depth.

## How to tell a real saving from a false one

A lower number from another country is only a saving if everything else stays the same. Before you act on one, check these points:

- **Same item.** The same hotel, dates and rate conditions; the same product model and variant; the same seat section.

- **Full total.** Taxes, service fees, resort fees, shipping and import duties all included.

- **Currency.** The amount your card is charged after conversion and any foreign transaction fee.

- **Eligibility.** Some rates are restricted to residents of a country, and some subscriptions can only be used where they were bought.

- **Terms.** Cancellation, return and warranty terms can differ between countries and sellers.

TipIf a price from another country looks far lower than everything else, check the rate conditions first. A breakfast-included flexible rate and a non-refundable room-only rate are different purchases, even at the same hotel.

## The challenge of manual arbitrage

People who discover that prices differ by country often try a VPN: connect to a server in another country, check the price, switch, check again. It is slow, many VPN addresses are recognized by sites, and it covers a handful of countries at best. Searching all 195 countries by hand is impossible in practice, and repeating that search every time the price might have changed is even less realistic. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is geographic price arbitrage?

It is buying the identical item at a lower price that is available from another country. The hotel, product or ticket stays the same; the country you search from changes, and with it the channels, local rates and promotions you can reach.

### Is geographic price arbitrage legal?

Searching for the same item from other countries is a form of shopping around, and the prices found can be booked or bought. Check each offer for residency or usage restrictions, which some rates and subscriptions have.

### Does geographic arbitrage only work for hotels?

No. Online shopping, event tickets, flights, car rentals and subscriptions also have prices that vary by country.

### What is the difference between live arbitrage and multiple-time arbitrage?

Live arbitrage searches the same item from many countries at checkout, in real time. Multiple-time arbitrage repeats the search after you buy, until the cancellation or return window ends, and the original is cancelled only once the new purchase is confirmed.
