Hotel price discrimination by location: how it really works

Hotels sell to each country differently. Here is what that means, why a VPN alone rarely changes the price, and how to use market pricing to pay less.

Price discrimination sounds sinister, but in economics it is a neutral term: selling the same product to different customers at different prices. Airlines do it with fare classes, software companies do it with country pricing, and hotels do it by market. If you have ever seen the same hotel at different prices when booked from different countries, you have seen hotel price discrimination by location.

What most people get wrong is the mechanism. The common story is that a booking site reads your IP address and raises the price. The reality is more interesting, and more useful if you want to pay less.

What price discrimination by location means for hotels

The travel industry calls it pricing by point of sale: the market where the booking is made. A hotel decides how it wants to sell to travelers from each country. It may offer a promotion to one market, sell through tour operators in another and leave a third to the big booking sites. Each of those decisions creates a different set of offers for the same hotel, depending on where the booking is made.

The price difference therefore comes from what is on offer in each country: different countries get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions.

Hotel prices by IP address: what actually happens

If you open the same hotel on the same site and change only your IP address, the price for the same rate usually moves very little, if at all. Travelers who test this with a VPN are often disappointed for exactly that reason. The real differences appear when changing countries changes what you can book:

  • Market-targeted rates. Booking sites let hotels create rates that are offered only to travelers booking from specific countries. These rates sit next to the standard rate and appear only in their target markets.
  • Local sellers. In many countries, local online agencies, tour operators and travel portals sell hotel stays from wholesale inventory. They do not show up in a search from your own country, but they can be cheaper for the identical hotel.
  • Local promotions. Payment partners, banks and local campaigns run discounts in one country and not in others.
  • Member pricing by market. Loyalty and member discounts are not offered the same way in every country.
  • Currency and tax display. Prices set in local currency drift with exchange rates, and some countries show totals with taxes while others add them at the end. Part of what looks like discrimination is just display.

That is also why the VPN method has limits, which we cover in VPN vs Arbitrica for hotel booking. The saving comes from finding the offers that exist in other countries.

How large location-based price differences get

In a test of 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, 80% of countries showed a lower price than the traveler's own country, with a typical gap of 20-40%. A 2024 NordVPN study found that travel bookings made through US websites came out up to 70% higher than through websites elsewhere (its largest gap was a car rental). One of its examples was a seven-night stay in a triple room at a Milan hotel for a family of three: about $1,216 searched from the US and $1,012 searched from Italy.

The country with the lowest price is not fixed. It changes from hotel to hotel and from week to week, as promotions start and end and as local demand moves.

Why hotel prices vary by country

Hotels have ordinary business reasons for pricing differently by market.

Feeder markets and seasons

Every hotel has markets that send most of its guests and markets it wants to grow. School holidays, public holidays and travel seasons fall at different times in different countries, so a hotel may push a discount toward a market whose travelers book during its quiet weeks.

Distribution contracts

Hotels sign deals with wholesalers and tour operators who buy inventory at negotiated rates and resell it in their own countries, often inside packages. Those rates follow their own rules and are not always matched on the big global sites.

Competition and purchasing power

What travelers from a given country are used to paying, and how many alternatives they see, influences how a hotel prices for that market. This is the reason behind "book from a cheaper country" advice. It is only one factor, though, and often not the decisive one, as the guide to the cheapest country to book hotels from explains.

Parity rules

For years, large booking sites used parity clauses that limited how much lower a hotel could price elsewhere. Several European countries restricted those clauses, and since Booking.com was designated a gatekeeper under the EU Digital Markets Act in 2024, it can no longer impose them in the EU. Looser parity gives hotels more scope to price differently by channel and market.

Is it legal and fair?

Pricing by market is common in travel and across many industries, and booking a rate that is openly offered is a normal purchase. The rules that matter to you are the rate conditions: if a rate is limited to residents of a country, the hotel can ask for proof at check-in.

Inside the European Union, the 2018 Geo-blocking Regulation limits traders from applying different general conditions, including prices, to customers from other EU countries purely because of their nationality or place of residence, and hotel accommodation is one of the services it covers. Outside the EU, rules vary.

How to use location-based pricing to pay less

  1. Fix your baselineStart with the exact hotel, dates, guests and rate type, and note the final total with taxes and fees.
  2. Search from other countriesSearch the same hotel from other countries, including local sellers where you can, not only the same global site.
  3. Skip resident-only ratesLeave them out unless you qualify.
  4. Compare totalsCompare after taxes, fees and currency conversion.
  5. Prefer free cancellationThe lowest country today may not be the lowest next week, so keep the option to rebook.

The hard part is coverage. There are 195 countries, the lowest price moves between them, and checking more than a handful by hand is impractical.

Frequently asked questions

What is hotel price discrimination by location?

It is the practice of selling the same hotel at different prices depending on the market where the booking is made. It happens through market-targeted rates, local sellers, local promotions and member pricing that differ by country.

Do hotel prices change by IP address?

On the same site, changing only your IP address usually moves the price of the same rate very little. Real differences come from the different rates and sellers available when you search from another country.

Why do hotel prices vary by country?

Hotels sell to each market differently: they target promotions at certain countries, sell through local agencies and wholesalers, and react to local seasons and competition. Currency and tax display add further differences.

Is it legal to book a hotel rate offered in another country?

Booking a rate that is openly offered is a normal purchase. Check the rate conditions, because some rates are limited to residents and the hotel can ask for proof at check-in.
