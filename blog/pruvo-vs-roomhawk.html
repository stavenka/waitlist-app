Pruvo and RoomHawk are two names that come up whenever travelers look for a way to pay less for a hotel after booking. Both were built around the same idea: prices keep moving after you reserve, so a booking with free cancellation can be watched and rebooked if the rate drops. They have since gone in different directions. Pruvo now sells to travel companies, while RoomHawk tracks bookings for individual travelers. This guide explains how each one works today, where Arbitrica fits, and which approach suits which kind of traveler or company.

How Pruvo works now: a B2B platform for travel companies

Pruvo started in 2016 as a consumer service. You forwarded your hotel confirmation email, Pruvo tracked the rate on the platform where you booked, and it emailed you when the price dropped so you could cancel and rebook. That model is covered in detail in our Arbitrica vs Pruvo comparison.

Today Pruvo's homepage calls it "the AI layer for the travel industry" and "a Web Travel Group company." Web Travel Group, which runs the WebBeds accommodation marketplace, agreed to acquire it, and PhocusWire reported that consumer bookings are no longer supported. The customers Pruvo lists are bedbanks, online travel agencies, destination management companies and tour operators.

Its live post-booking product, which Pruvo calls Reshop, watches confirmed reservations until check-in and rebooks the same hotel at a lower price inside the operator's policy. Pruvo's product page describes the order as "new confirmed, old canceled, never the other way," with an optional human check for high-value moves. Pruvo says the recovered margin is posted to the operator's ledger, so the saving goes to the travel company.

So when people search "Pruvo alternative," they are usually in one of two groups: travelers who used the old consumer service and want something that does the same job, or companies weighing Pruvo's platform against other ways to cut hotel spend.

How RoomHawk works: post-booking alerts for travelers

RoomHawk is the closer match to Pruvo's original consumer service. According to its about page, you enter your reservation and RoomHawk "instantly launches the ai search," tracking identical free-cancellation options across top booking sites. When it detects a lower price, it sends you an alert. You click the link, cancel your existing booking and rebook at the lower rate yourself. RoomHawk's about page also says the service is currently in beta.

That makes RoomHawk a good fit for a traveler who already has a refundable booking and wants a second pair of eyes on the price. It does not change what you pay at the moment you book, and it leaves the rebooking steps to you. RoomHawk does not describe searching the hotel from other countries. We cover it in more depth in Arbitrica vs RoomHawk.

The mechanism both trackers share: time arbitrage

Pruvo and RoomHawk work on the same source of savings: price movement over time. A hotel's revenue management system changes rates as demand shifts, as other guests cancel and as arrival gets closer. If your booking can be cancelled without a penalty, every drop before the deadline is a chance to book the same hotel again for less.

Time arbitrage is real and worth doing. It has two limits. It only starts after you have already paid, so the first price you lock in is whatever you happened to find. And it usually watches the price from one country, the one you booked from, so it only sees one set of rates for that hotel.

What both leave out: the same hotel from other countries

The same hotel, on the same dates and terms, can be offered at different prices depending on which country you search from. Different countries get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions, so searching the same hotel from another country opens up lower prices you cannot get from your own country.

In a test of 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, 80% of countries showed a lower price than the traveler's own country, and the typical gap was 20-40%. That gap exists at the same moment, before any price drop. A tracker that watches one country's price over time never sees it.

You can check a few countries by hand with a VPN, one at a time, but covering 195 countries for every booking is not practical. That is the gap Arbitrica was built to close.

How Arbitrica differs from Pruvo and RoomHawk

Arbitrica is a personal AI agent that meets you at checkout. When you are about to pay for a hotel, it searches the same hotel from 195 countries and finds the lowest price on Earth for it, at a different provider from the one you are on. Before that price counts, it validates that the hotel and terms are identical and that all taxes and fees are included. Then it prepares everything, so you only review and pay.

After you book, Arbitrica's approach repeats the search over time until the free-cancellation window ends. This is the part that overlaps with Pruvo and RoomHawk, with one difference: each repeat is searched from 195 countries rather than one. When the identical hotel becomes available at a lower price, a new booking is prepared, and the new booking is confirmed before the original is cancelled.

In one real booking, the hotel was offered at $1,527.39 on Booking.com. Arbitrica found the identical hotel for $1,180.27 at another provider, earning the user $347.12 (23%). The same mechanisms apply beyond hotels, to flights, car rentals, event tickets and online shopping, and to anything with a cancellation or return window.

How to pick a hotel price tracker

Whichever service you use, ask the same questions:

  1. Does it act before you pay?A tracker that only works after booking cannot improve the first price you lock in.
  2. Where does the search run from?One country sees one set of prices. More countries means more chances at a lower total.
  3. Does it check the full price?A lower nightly rate with new fees added is not a lower price. Compare totals with all taxes and fees.
  4. Is it the identical hotel and terms?Same hotel, same dates, same board and the same cancellation policy, or it is not a like-for-like saving.
  5. Who does the rebooking?An alert is a to-do. A prepared booking with the new reservation confirmed before the old one is cancelled is less work and less risk.
  6. Who gets the saving?A B2B platform saves the travel company money. A consumer service saves you money.

Tip

Any post-booking tracker only works on refundable rates. A non-refundable booking cannot be rebooked, however far the price falls, so a flexible rate that costs a little more on day one is often the better deal.

Frequently asked questions

Is Pruvo still available for travelers?

No. Pruvo now describes itself as a platform for the travel industry, owned by Web Travel Group, and consumer bookings are no longer supported. Its customers are travel companies such as bedbanks, OTAs and tour operators.

What is the difference between Pruvo and RoomHawk?

Pruvo rebooks hotels automatically for travel companies to improve their margin. RoomHawk tracks a traveler's own refundable booking and sends an alert when the price drops, leaving the rebooking to the traveler.

What is a good Pruvo alternative?

For post-booking alerts, RoomHawk is the closest match to Pruvo's old consumer service. Arbitrica searches the same hotel from 195 countries at checkout, repeats the search after booking and prepares the rebooking for you.

Does RoomHawk rebook my hotel for me?

According to RoomHawk's about page, it sends you an alert with a link, and you cancel your existing booking and rebook at the lower rate yourself.

Can companies use Arbitrica?

Yes. Arbitrica serves companies as well as travelers, including teams traveling to trade shows and conferences, operations managers and travel managers.
