If you are looking for the Hotels.com price guarantee, the short answer is that it no longer exists. Expedia Group ended the Hotel Price Guarantee on both Hotels.com and Expedia on July 28, 2026, and no replacement has been announced for hotels. This guide explains what the guarantee covered, what changed, which price matches still exist, and how to get a lower price for the same hotel without one.

Does Hotels.com still have a price guarantee?

No. In June 2026, Frequent Miler reported a Hotels.com email to members that said: "We will no longer be offering Hotel Price Guarantee on Expedia or Hotels.com." AwardWallet reported the same change with an effective date of July 28, 2026, and Upgraded Points, in its coverage of the One Key changes that went live that day, wrote that both sites "have eliminated the Hotel Price Guarantee" and that "there's no replacement for it."

Hotels.com and Expedia are both owned by Expedia Group and share the One Key rewards program, which is why the change applies to both sites at once. Some older Hotels.com and Expedia help pages can still turn up in search results with guarantee wording; check the date and the current terms on the site before relying on them.

Tip

If you booked before July 28, 2026 and want to make a claim, contact Hotels.com customer service directly. The public reports on the change do not describe how bookings made before that date are handled, so get the answer from Hotels.com in writing.

What the Hotels.com price guarantee used to do

According to Upgraded Points, the Hotel Price Guarantee paid members who found a lower price after booking. Like most guarantees of this kind, it was built around a claim: you found the lower offer, submitted it, and the site checked whether it qualified before paying the difference.

That check is where most claims succeed or fail. Price guarantees across the travel industry share a few common conditions:

  • The lower offer must be for the same hotel, dates, number of guests and room type.
  • The cancellation policy and board, such as breakfast included, must match.
  • The offer must be publicly available, so member-only, corporate and coupon rates usually do not count.
  • There is a deadline for submitting the claim, often tied to check-in.

Each program writes its own version of these rules, and the site that pays the claim decides whether your offer meets them.

How to claim a hotel price guarantee where one still exists

If you booked on a site or with a hotel chain that still offers a price match, the steps are similar everywhere:

  1. Read the current termsFind the guarantee page on the site you booked with and note the deadline and the exclusions.
  2. Match every detailSame hotel, dates, guests, room type, board and cancellation policy. Check the total with all taxes and fees on both offers.
  3. Capture the evidenceTake screenshots of the lower offer showing the price, dates, room type and terms, with the date and time visible.
  4. Submit quicklyPrices move, and if the lower offer disappears before the claim is checked, it may be rejected. On Booking.com, NerdWallet says you can claim from your reservation confirmation or through customer care.
  5. Keep the referenceSave the claim number and any replies until the adjustment is applied.

The limit of every price guarantee: "the same offer"

A price guarantee only pays when the site agrees that the lower price is for exactly the same offer. That definition is written by the site paying the claim. A different room name, a slightly different cancellation policy, a rate that requires signing in, or an offer from a site that does not reveal the hotel until after booking can each be enough to reject a claim. NerdWallet's summary of Booking.com's rules, for example, lists loyalty-only rates, offers labeled "Partner offer" and sites that do not reveal the property until after booking among the exclusions.

A guarantee also depends on you finding the lower price first. It does not search for you, and it only helps with the site you booked on. Lower prices for the same hotel are often found at other providers and from other countries, where no guarantee on your original site applies.

A price guarantee covers what a site considers the same offer. The lower price is often somewhere it does not look.

Why the same hotel can be lower when searched from another country

Different countries get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions. Searching the same hotel from another country can open up lower prices that you cannot get from your own country, often from a different provider.

In a test of 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, 80% of countries showed a lower price than the traveler's own country, and the typical gap was 20-40%. Independent research points the same way: a 2024 NordVPN study found that travel bookings made through US websites came out up to 70% higher than through websites elsewhere (its largest gap was a car rental).

In that study, a seven-night stay in a triple room at a Milan hotel for a family of three cost about $1,216 searched from the US and $1,012 searched from Italy. No price guarantee would pay that difference, because the lower offer comes from a different search and, often, a different provider. Frequently asked questions

Does Hotels.com still have a price guarantee?

No. Expedia Group ended the Hotel Price Guarantee on both Hotels.com and Expedia on July 28, 2026, and reports at the time said no replacement had been announced.

Does Hotels.com price match?

Not since the Hotel Price Guarantee ended on July 28, 2026. If your booking has free cancellation, you can book the lower rate yourself and cancel the original once the new booking is confirmed.

Does Expedia Price Drop Protection cover hotels?

No. Expedia's page describes One Key Price Drop Protection for flights booked in the Expedia app by One Key Gold and Platinum members, paid as OneKeyCash after the trip.

Which hotel booking sites still offer a price match?

Booking.com still publishes a price match with its own conditions, and many hotel chains offer best rate guarantees for direct bookings. Read the current terms before you rely on any of them.
