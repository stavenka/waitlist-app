What Alexa for Shopping does

Amazon announced Alexa for Shopping on May 13, 2026, bringing together Rufus and Alexa+ for US customers in the Amazon Shopping app, on the website and on Echo Show devices. TechCrunch reported that it replaces Rufus, the shopping assistant Amazon launched in 2024, and Amazon says Rufus helped over 300 million customers in 2025. According to Amazon (checked on November 21, 2026), it can:

  • Track prices: set price alerts and view up to a full year of price history on hundreds of millions of products.
  • Buy at a target price: Amazon lists buying items at your target price; its example is asking it to add sunscreen to your cart if the price drops to $10.
  • Compare: select several products from search results and see them side by side, with shopping guides that cover features, prices and reviews across Amazon and the web.
  • Scheduled Actions: recurring or one-time tasks, after which it notifies you or adds items to your cart so you review and check out.
  • Buy for Me: for eligible products, it handles the entire purchase on your behalf using your primary address and credit card.

When Amazon began testing Buy for Me with a subset of users, TechCrunch reported on April 3, 2025 that it shows products other websites sell when Amazon does not sell what you searched for, and that the agent visits the external site, selects the product and fills in your name, shipping address and payment details. Returns and exchanges go through the store the agent bought from. eMarketer describes Amazon retiring the Rufus brand as it joins the chatbot with Alexa+.

How Alexa for Shopping gets the price

Alexa for Shopping works from Amazon's store in the US. Its price history and target-price buying follow the price Amazon shows you, and Buy for Me pays the price on the other store's site. Amazon's announcement does not describe searching the same product from other countries or other countries' Amazon stores (checked on November 21, 2026).

That matters because the same product is often priced differently depending on where the search comes from. Retailers agree prices, stock and promotions market by market, and dynamic pricing moves them all day; see why Amazon prices differ between countries. Hotels show the same pattern: in Arbitrica's own price check, a 4-night stay at White House Hotel Istanbul cost $1,066 searched from the UK and $708 searched from India, 33.6% less, for the same hotel and the same dates.

How Arbitrica handles the price

Arbitrica is a personal AI agent that shows up after you choose what you are buying or your hotel room. For the product, hotel or event ticket you have chosen, it:

  • searches the same item from 195 countries, on the site you are on and other sites;
  • checks that it is identical (the same product, or the same hotel and room) and that the final total includes all taxes and fees;
  • finds the lowest price in the world for it and automatically prepares everything in a new tab, so you only review and pay;
  • keeps searching after you buy or book until the return or free-cancellation window ends, as an approach Arbitrica uses, and confirms any new booking before the original is cancelled.

Doing the same by hand means switching your location country by country and repeating every search. Across 195 countries that is humanly impossible, which is the job Arbitrica does. Research demonstrated an average of $34 per online purchase, $47 per hotel booking and $107 per event ticket purchase with Arbitrica.
