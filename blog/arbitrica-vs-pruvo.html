Arbitrica vs. Pruvo: which saves you more?

Pruvo tracked one booking on one platform from one country, and it now serves businesses only. Here is how the two approaches differ.

Pruvo was one of the best-known hotel price trackers for travelers who wanted to catch price drops after booking. Arbitrica works on the same problem from a different direction. This comparison looks at how each one finds a lower price, where the saving comes from, and how much of the work is left to you.

Pruvo has since moved away from consumer bookings and now presents itself as a business-to-business platform (see the update at the end of this article). So this comparison covers both sides: travelers who used Pruvo, or a tracker built on the same model, and companies weighing Pruvo's business offering. Arbitrica serves both consumers and companies.

How Pruvo works

Pruvo monitored hotel prices after you booked. You forwarded your booking confirmation email to check@pruvo.com, and Pruvo began tracking the rate. If the price dropped on the same platform, Pruvo sent you an alert. You then cancelled your old reservation and rebooked at the lower rate yourself.

Pruvo offered basic tracking at no charge, and a premium tier added priority monitoring and multi-booking management. The system was straightforward and did what it promised. Today Pruvo describes itself as an AI platform for travel companies, built around rebooking hotel reservations when prices fall.

How Arbitrica works

Arbitrica is a personal AI agent that lives in your browser. It meets you at the checkout when you are about to pay for a hotel, searches the same hotel from 195 countries, and finds the lowest price on Earth for it, at a different seller from the one you are on. It then prepares the booking, so you only review and pay.

The approach rests on two families of mechanisms:

  • Geographic arbitrage. Different countries get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions. Searching the same hotel from another country can open up lower prices you cannot get from your own country.
  • Time arbitrage. Live arbitrage runs the search at checkout, in real time. Multiple-time arbitrage repeats the search after you book, until the free-cancellation window ends, and prepares a re-booking at the lower price. The new booking is confirmed before the original is cancelled.

Before a lower price counts, Arbitrica validates that it is for the identical hotel, that it includes all taxes and fees, and that it will not suddenly change at the payment step.

The core difference: where the saving comes from

Pruvo checked for price drops in the same country and on the same platform where you originally booked. If you booked from the US on Booking.com, Pruvo monitored the US Booking.com price. That catches drops over time, but it never looks at the prices available from other countries.

Arbitrica starts from the other side. It searches the same hotel from 195 countries before you pay, so the price you book is already the lowest it could find. The same hotel is often 5-30% cheaper when searched from another country. In a test of 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, 80% of countries showed a lower price than the traveler's own country. Doing that by hand, with a VPN and one country at a time, is impossible in practice. Arbitrica's multiple-time approach then covers the drops over time that Pruvo was built for, searched from 195 countries instead of one.

Setup and user experience

Pruvo required you to forward each booking confirmation email. If you forgot to forward a booking, Pruvo did not track it. When it found a lower rate, you got an email, and then had to go back to the booking platform, cancel the old reservation and rebook at the new rate yourself.

Arbitrica works at the moment of payment. Because it is there at the checkout, there is nothing to forward. When it finds a lower price for the identical hotel, it prepares everything, so your part is to review and pay. The multiple-time approach works the same way: the re-booking at the lower price is prepared for you, and the new booking is confirmed before the original is cancelled, instead of landing in your inbox as a to-do.

Rebooking by hand safely, whichever route you take

If you still track prices yourself, a few habits stop a price drop from turning into a problem:

  1. Book a refundable rateA drop after booking is only useful if you can cancel the original without a penalty.
  2. Compare totals, not nightly ratesTaxes, resort fees and payment fees can wipe out a small headline drop.
  3. Match the terms exactlySame hotel, dates, guests, meal plan and cancellation policy.
  4. Book the new one firstCancel the original only after the new confirmation arrives, so you are never left without a hotel.

Beyond hotels

Pruvo was built for hotel bookings only. Arbitrica's mechanisms are not limited to hotels. Searching the same item from 195 countries also works for flights, car rentals, event tickets and online shopping such as Amazon orders. Repeating the search over time works for anything with a cancellation or return window, including pre-orders and subscriptions.

For companies: Arbitrica vs. Pruvo's B2B model

With Pruvo now positioned as a business-to-business platform, the comparison matters for companies as much as for individual travelers. This article does not cover the details of Pruvo's business offering, so check its current terms directly. The model it was built on, tracking a booking for drops over time on one platform from one country, is the part that can be compared.

Arbitrica works for companies as well as consumers. Three groups get the most from it:

  • Teams traveling to trade shows and conferences. Many people book hotels in the same city for the same dates, when hotel prices near the venue tend to rise. Each of those bookings is searched from 195 countries at the checkout, and event hotels are often booked early with free cancellation, which leaves a long window for the search to be repeated before the trip.
  • Operations managers who book travel for staff. They get the lowest price Arbitrica finds for the identical hotel, validated with all taxes and fees, without checking countries one at a time.
  • Travel managers responsible for hotel spend across the company. The traveler stays in the hotel they chose, on the same dates and terms, and only the price changes.

The mechanisms are the same as for a single traveler. For a company with many bookings a year, a single-country tracker leaves the larger source of price variation untouched. Read more in business travel hotel savings for travel managers and trade show hotel prices.

Which is better?

Pruvo was a solid choice for travelers who wanted simple post-booking alerts on one platform. It caught drops over time reliably and was easy to set up. It no longer serves consumers.

Arbitrica covers more ground, for travelers and companies alike. It searches the same hotel from 195 countries before you pay, validates that the lower price is for the identical hotel with all taxes and fees, and prepares the booking for you. Its multiple-time approach repeats that search after you book. In one real booking, Booking.com offered $1,527.39 and Arbitrica found $1,180.27 for the identical hotel, earning the user $347.12 (23%). A single-country tracker never sees a price like that. Research showed that people earn on average $47 per hotel booking with Arbitrica, and up to $3,000 a year.
