Is it cheaper to buy from another country online?

Sometimes by a lot. What decides it is the total you pay once shipping, import taxes and card fees are added.

Often, yes. Online prices are different in different countries, sometimes by a little and sometimes by a lot. Whether you actually come out ahead depends on what you are buying and on the total you pay once shipping, taxes and fees are added. This guide goes through the main categories, what is legitimate and practical, and what to check before you buy.

Why online prices differ between countries

Geo pricing in e-commerce is normal. A store selling into many countries rarely uses one global price, for several reasons:

  • Taxes in the sticker price. In the EU and the UK, consumer prices usually include VAT. In the US, the listed price usually excludes sales tax, which is added at checkout. The same product can look more expensive in Europe simply because tax is already in the number.
  • Local competition. A product competing with strong local rivals is often priced lower in that market.
  • Distribution and inventory. Different countries get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions. A national store may have bought stock at a different cost or run its own sales calendar.
  • Currency. Prices set in local currency drift as exchange rates move, and stores do not reprice every day.
  • Purchasing power. Digital goods in particular are often priced lower in countries with lower average incomes.

Electronics and physical goods

This is where people most often ask whether it is cheaper to buy from another country. The answer turns on landed cost. A laptop that is $90 lower on a foreign store can end up more expensive once international shipping, import duty and import VAT or GST are added, plus a currency conversion fee on your card. Warranty is the other trap: some manufacturers only honor warranty in the country of purchase, which means sending the product abroad if it fails. Check the manufacturer's warranty terms before you buy.

Subscriptions and software

Streaming and software prices vary widely by country, and that is well documented. But many services tie your account to the country where you live and check your payment method and location. Signing up in another country can break those terms and lead to a suspended account. The legitimate wins here are usually local promotions in your own country, annual plans, and buying software from a reseller that is authorized to sell in your country.

Games

Digital game stores commonly set different prices by country, and many restrict keys or purchases to the country or area they were sold in. Keys bought elsewhere may simply not activate. Treat very low prices from unofficial key sellers with suspicion.

Flights

Airline fares can differ depending on the country the ticket is sold in and the currency you pay in. The gap is sometimes worth it, especially on long-haul routes. Watch for card fees on foreign currency and make sure the fare rules, such as changes and baggage, are the same as the fare you were comparing against.

Amazon price in a different country: what actually happens

Amazon runs separate national stores, such as amazon.com, amazon.co.uk and amazon.de, each with its own prices, sellers and promotions. The same product can have a noticeably different price on two of them. Many items on these stores can ship internationally, and some show an estimate of import fees at checkout so you see more of the total before paying. Others do not ship to your country at all. More detail is in Amazon prices in different countries.

When you compare Amazon stores, line up the same thing:

  1. The exact same product, model number and variant.
  2. The same seller type: sold by Amazon or by a third-party seller.
  3. Shipping to your address, not to the store's home country.
  4. Import duty and taxes for your country.
  5. Your card's foreign transaction fee, if any.
  6. How returns work from abroad.

A worked example of landed cost (illustrative)

Here is how the math tends to go. The numbers are made up to show the method, not real quotes.

A $100 gap on the sticker price turned into an $11 loss. Change one or two lines, such as a store that ships at no extra charge or a card with no foreign fees, and the same purchase could save you money. That is why the total is the only number that matters.

Tip

Before you buy from a foreign store, add up item, shipping, import duty and taxes, and your card's foreign fee in one line. If the total is not clearly lower, the warranty and returns risk is rarely worth it.

What is legitimate, and what is not

  • Legitimate: buying a physical product from a store in another country and paying the import taxes your country charges.
  • Legitimate: booking a hotel, flight or ticket at a price available from another country, as long as you meet the booking's terms.
  • Risky: signing up for subscriptions or game stores in a country you do not live in, when the terms require residence.
  • Not legitimate: under-declaring the value of goods at customs to avoid duty.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to buy from another country online?

Often, but not always. The sticker price can be lower abroad while shipping, import duty, taxes and card fees make the total higher. Compare the full total for the identical item.

Why are online prices different in different countries?

Taxes included or excluded in the listed price, local competition, different inventory, channels and promotions, currency movements and purchasing power all lead stores to price the same item differently by country.

Is the Amazon price different in another country?

Yes. Amazon runs separate national stores with their own prices, sellers and promotions, so the same product can cost more or less on each one.

What should I watch for when buying from abroad?

Shipping, import duty and VAT, warranty terms, plug and voltage differences, card foreign transaction fees, returns from abroad, and any terms that tie digital services to your country of residence.
