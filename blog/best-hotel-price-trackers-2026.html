Best Hotel Price Trackers in 2026

The 8 main options compared, what each is good at, and the decision rule for picking the right one.

Last updated October 2026 · Arbitrica Team

Short version: Eight options in the category and three things that matter: a search at checkout, a search after booking, and how many countries the search covers. The decision rule: pick the one that searches the same hotel from other countries, because that is where the largest price differences are. Arbitrica searches from all 195.

Why does a hotel price tracker matter in 2026?

Short answer: Hotel pricing is more dynamic and more opaque than at any point since online travel agencies arrived. The same hotel can carry several different prices on the same night depending on the site, the currency, the member tier and the country you search from. Checking all of them by hand is not realistic. A hotel price tracker does that work for you, before or after you book.

The online travel agency model took off in the late 1990s, and pricing has only become more complex since. Most major chains run revenue-management systems that adjust the rate every few hours based on remaining inventory, search volume and competitor rates. Different countries also get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions. The number on the screen when you book is rarely the lowest price available for that hotel.

A hotel price tracker watches some or all of this on your behalf. The category now has three sub-types: tools that look for a lower price for the same hotel before you pay; post-booking monitors that check for price drops after you have reserved; and full-flow agents that do both and prepare the rebooking for you.

What did we evaluate?

Short answer: Each option is reviewed against four criteria: search at checkout, search after booking, country coverage and automatic rebooking.

The four criteria: search at checkout (does it find a lower price for the same hotel before you book?), search after booking (does it watch for drops after you book?), country coverage (from how many countries does it search?), and automatic rebooking (does it handle the cancellation and re-reservation, or do you?). All eight were active when this guide was first published; Pruvo has since left the consumer market, as noted below.

1. Arbitrica

Arbitrica is a personal AI agent that lives in your browser and shows up at the checkout when you are about to pay, searches the same hotel from 195 countries, and finds the lowest price on Earth for it, no matter who sells it. Different countries get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions, so the same hotel searched from another country is often 5-30% cheaper. Arbitrica validates that the lower price is for the identical hotel, includes all taxes and fees, and will not suddenly change, then automatically prepares the booking so you only review and pay.

Its multiple-time arbitrage approach repeats the search after you book, until the free-cancellation window ends, and prepares a re-booking at the lower price. The new booking is confirmed before the original is cancelled. The same mechanisms apply beyond hotels, to flights, event tickets, online shopping and anything else with a cancellation or return window.

Best for: anyone who wants the lowest price on a hotel with the least effort, from individual travelers to companies: teams traveling to trade shows and conferences, operations managers and travel managers.

Limitation: runs in Chrome for now; more options are coming soon. Works on cash bookings, not award redemptions. For award travel use Seats.aero or Roame.

2. Pruvo

Pruvo is a post-booking tracker. You forward your booking confirmation by email, Pruvo parses the property and dates, and checks the price on the same booking platform every few hours. When it finds a drop, you cancel and rebook manually. The product has been around since 2017 and the email-based onboarding is genuinely easy.

Limitation: single country, no geographic search. Manual rebooking only. Pruvo also misses chain-direct rates and member discounts.

Update: Web Travel Group has announced its acquisition of Pruvo, which now presents itself as a business-to-business platform with consumer bookings no longer supported. Arbitrica works for both consumers and companies, so travelers and businesses alike have an option that searches the same hotel from 195 countries.

3. RoomHawk

RoomHawk tracks 2 million+ hotel rooms for post-booking price drops, sends clean email alerts, and charges a percentage of confirmed savings. The interface is one of the better ones in the category and the company has a long-running track record. Most user-reported savings cluster in the $50-$300 range per qualifying booking.

Limitation: single country, no search at checkout, manual rebooking required. RoomHawk also lacks coverage on chain direct sites for several major brands.

For the full RoomHawk-vs-Arbitrica comparison see our side-by-side review.

4. RatePunk

RatePunk is a browser extension focused on comparing platforms before checkout. When you reach a hotel listing on Booking.com, Expedia or Hotels.com, RatePunk overlays the same hotel’s price on the other platforms. The extension is free and the team adds new booking sites often. For users who only want to know whether the same hotel is listed lower on another site right now, RatePunk is a clean experience.

Limitation: platform comparison only, from one country. RatePunk does not search the same hotel from other countries, where it is often 5-30% cheaper, and it does not monitor after booking.

5. Repriced

Repriced is one of the few post-booking trackers that handles rebooking automatically. Once you forward your confirmation, Repriced monitors the price and, if a drop appears, performs the cancellation and re-reservation without involving you. Repriced takes 25% of confirmed savings.

Limitation: the 25% fee comes out of every saving. It searches from only a few countries, so most of the price differences between countries are missed, and there is no search at checkout.

6. OneAir

OneAir is a travel subscription that bundles hotel price tracking with flight deal alerts, airport lounge passes and other perks. The hotel component watches for post-booking price drops and emails users when one appears. The subscription is $59.99/year.

Limitation: hotel tracking is one feature among many, and its depth is not at the level of single-purpose trackers. No geographic search. Manual rebooking required.

7. Hotel Ninja

Hotel Ninja focuses on simplicity. Its free tier lets you track unlimited bookings with email alerts when prices drop. The product has a limited marketing presence but solid uptime and a small loyal user base.

Limitation: single country. No search at checkout. Manual rebooking. Monitoring only starts once you have booked.

8. Directo

Directo compares hotel direct-booking sites against the OTA price, looking for cases where booking through the chain’s own website is cheaper than going through Booking.com or Expedia. For loyalty-program members this is sometimes the case (member rates, free breakfast, late checkout), and Directo shows the difference.

Limitation: narrow focus on direct-vs-OTA comparison. No post-booking monitoring. No geographic search. The saving is capped at the gap between the direct site and the OTA, which is usually 5-10% on chains.

Which hotel price tracker saves the most money?

Short answer: the one that searches the same hotel from the most countries. Without geographic search, the most any tracker can find is a drop over time on one country’s listing. With it, the same hotel is often 5-30% cheaper from another country before any drop happens. Arbitrica searches from 195 countries at checkout and repeats the search over time.

Ranked by the mechanisms each one uses, from the widest to the narrowest:

  1. Arbitrica: the same hotel from 195 countries at checkout (live arbitrage), plus the multiple-time approach after booking, with the booking prepared for you.
  2. Repriced: drops over time from a few countries, with automatic rebooking.
  3. RoomHawk, Pruvo, Hotel Ninja, OneAir: drops over time from one country, rebooking left to you.
  4. RatePunk, Directo: one-moment differences between platforms or between the direct site and the OTA.

Common questions about hotel price trackers

Does Arbitrica work before or after I book?

Both. Live arbitrage runs at the checkout, when you are about to pay: Arbitrica searches the same hotel from 195 countries and finds the lowest price on Earth for it. Its multiple-time approach repeats the search after you book, until the free-cancellation window ends, and prepares a re-booking at the lower price. The new booking is confirmed before the original is cancelled.

What if the lower price is in a different currency?

Arbitrica validates that the lower price is for the identical hotel, that it includes all taxes and fees, and that it will not suddenly change before you pay. A price that only looks lower before everything is counted does not count.

Does Arbitrica only look at Booking.com and Expedia?

No. Arbitrica finds the lowest price for the identical hotel no matter who sells it: Booking, Expedia, the hotel chain or anyone else. The seller is never the point; the lowest price for the identical hotel, with all taxes and fees, is.

Is there a hotel price tracker that handles award (points/miles) redemptions?

Award redemptions follow different inventory and pricing rules that cash-price trackers do not see. Seats.aero and Roame are purpose-built for award travel; Arbitrica and the other options listed here are for cash bookings. If you book both, you will probably want both.

What is the difference between a hotel price tracker and Hopper?

Hopper is a metasearch and booking app that predicts price movement and offers price-freeze products. It does not monitor your existing bookings or rebook for you. Hopper predicts whether prices will drop; Arbitrica searches the same hotel from 195 countries for lower prices that already exist right now.
