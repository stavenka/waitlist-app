Why does a hotel price tracker matter in 2026?

Short answer: Hotel pricing is more dynamic and more opaque than at any point since OTAs were invented. The same room sells at 8-12 different prices on the same night across OTAs, countries, currencies, and member tiers. No human can compare all of them — software can. A hotel price tracker captures the differences automatically, before and after you book.

Hotel pricing is now more dynamic and more opaque than at any point since the OTA model was invented in the late 1990s. A single hotel room is sold at 8-12 different prices on the same night across OTAs, currencies, country IPs, and member-rate tiers. Behind the scenes, most major chains run revenue-management systems that adjust the rate every few hours based on remaining inventory, search volume, and competitor pricing. The number on the screen when you book is rarely the number you should have paid.

A hotel price tracker is software that monitors all of this on your behalf. The category has matured into three sub-types: pre-checkout comparison tools that show you the cheapest version of a room before you pay; post-booking monitors that check for price drops after you have reserved; and full-flow tools that do both and rebook automatically. The market is now large enough to compare seriously.

What did we evaluate?

Short answer: Each tool is reviewed against five criteria: pre-checkout search, post-booking monitoring, country-IP coverage, automatic rebooking, and pricing model. All eight tools are currently active in 2026. Where possible we cite measured savings data from real bookings.

Each tool below is reviewed against five criteria: pre-checkout search (does it find the cheapest version of a room before you book?), post-booking monitoring (does it watch for drops after you book?), country-IP coverage (how many regions does it query?), automatic rebooking (does it handle the cancellation and re-reservation, or do you?), and pricing model (free, subscription, performance fee). 1. Arbitrica

Arbitrica searches hotel prices across 200 countries simultaneously, both before and after you book. The Chrome extension detects your booking automatically. If a cheaper rate appears on a refundable room — whether through geographic arbitrage or a temporal drop — Arbitrica confirms the new reservation, cancels the original, and emails you the refund confirmation. The user does nothing. Free for the first 10,000 users; $20/month plus 10% of savings after that.

Best for: anyone who books international hotels and wants the largest savings with the least effort.

Limitation: works on cash bookings, not award redemptions. For award travel use Seats.aero or Roame.

2. Pruvo

Pruvo is a post-booking tracker. You forward your booking confirmation by email, Pruvo parses the property and dates, and checks the price on the same booking platform every few hours. When it finds a drop, you cancel and rebook manually. The product has been around since 2017 and the email-based onboarding is genuinely easy.

Limitation: single country, no geographic search. Manual rebooking only. Pruvo also misses chain-direct rates and member discounts.

3. RoomHawk

RoomHawk tracks 2 million+ hotel rooms for post-booking price drops, sends clean email alerts, and charges a percentage of confirmed savings. The interface is one of the better ones in the category and the company has a long-running track record. Most user-reported savings cluster in the $50-$300 range per qualifying booking.

Limitation: single country, no pre-checkout comparison, manual rebooking required. RoomHawk also lacks coverage on chain direct sites for several major brands.

For the full RoomHawk-vs-Arbitrica comparison see our side-by-side review.

4. RatePunk

RatePunk is a browser extension focused entirely on pre-checkout comparison. When you reach a hotel listing on Booking.com, Expedia, or Hotels.com, RatePunk overlays the same room's price on the other platforms. The extension is free and the team adds new OTAs frequently. For users who only want to know "is there a cheaper version of this room on another OTA right now," RatePunk is the cleanest experience.

Limitation: platform comparison only. Platform-vs-platform spreads typically run 3-8%; geographic spreads run 15-40%. RatePunk does not see the larger source of variation. It also does not monitor after booking.

5. Repriced

Repriced is one of the few post-booking trackers that handles rebooking automatically. Once you forward your confirmation, Repriced monitors the price and — if a drop appears — performs the cancellation and re-reservation without involving you. The model resembles Arbitrica's auto-rebook flow at a smaller geographic scale. Repriced takes 25% of confirmed savings.

Limitation: 25% performance fee is on the higher end. Country-IP coverage is limited (not 200 like Arbitrica), so geographic spreads are mostly missed.

6. OneAir

OneAir is a travel subscription that bundles hotel price tracking with flight deal alerts, airport lounge passes, and other perks. The hotel component watches for post-booking price drops and emails users when one appears. Subscription is $59.99/year.

Limitation: hotel tracking is one feature among many; the depth is not at the level of single-purpose trackers. No geographic search. Manual rebooking required.

7. Hotel Ninja

Hotel Ninja focuses on simplicity. The free tier lets you track unlimited bookings with email alerts when prices drop. The product has limited marketing presence but solid uptime and a small loyal user base.

Limitation: single country. No pre-checkout search. Manual rebooking. The free model means no proactive monitoring outside of post-booking.

8. Directo

Directo compares hotel direct-booking sites against the OTA price, looking for cases where booking through the chain's own website is cheaper than going through Booking.com or Expedia. For loyalty-program members this is sometimes the case (member rates, free breakfast, late checkout) and Directo surfaces the difference.

Limitation: narrow focus on direct-vs-OTA comparison. No post-booking monitoring. No geographic search. The savings ceiling is the direct-vs-OTA delta, which is usually 5-10% on chains.

Which hotel price tracker saves the most money?

Short answer: Arbitrica leads with 18.7% average savings per qualifying booking and 14% median. Repriced is second at 8-12% average (auto-rebooking helps). RoomHawk and Pruvo follow at 5-10%. The gap is driven by geographic search — without multi-country IP coverage, the saving ceiling is the temporal drop on a single country's OTA listing, which is structurally smaller.

Common questions about hotel price trackers

Do I need to keep my Chrome extension on for Arbitrica to work?

The extension only needs to be installed and signed in. The post-booking monitoring happens on Arbitrica's servers, not in your browser. The extension itself is needed for the pre-checkout search at the moment of booking.

What if the cheaper rate is in a different currency?

Arbitrica normalizes all rates to your home currency using the live mid-market exchange rate (we use Wise's rate as the benchmark). The cheaper rate is the cheaper rate after FX conversion, not before. Currency arbitrage is part of the larger geographic spread.

How does Arbitrica handle hotel chains like Marriott and Hilton?

Arbitrica searches both the chain's direct site and the major OTAs (Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, Agoda) for each property. For chains that offer member-rate discounts, we use the chain's anonymous public price by default. We do not log into your loyalty account because that would require credential sharing we do not want to ask for.

Is there a hotel price tracker that handles award (points/miles) redemptions?

Award redemptions follow different inventory and pricing rules that the standard scraping infrastructure does not see. Seats.aero and Roame are purpose-built for award travel; Arbitrica and the other tools listed here are for cash bookings. If you book both, you will probably want both.

What is the difference between a hotel price tracker and Hopper?

Hopper is a metasearch and booking app that predicts price movement and offers price-freeze products. It does not monitor your existing bookings or rebook automatically. The closest comparison is between Hopper's pre-checkout prediction feature and the pre-checkout search done by Arbitrica or RatePunk — but Hopper predicts whether prices will drop, while Arbitrica searches for cheaper versions that already exist in other markets.
