RoomHawk catches drops over time on the price you already see. Arbitrica searches the same hotel from 195 countries, before you pay and after.

RoomHawk and Arbitrica both help travelers pay less for hotels. RoomHawk focuses on watching a hotel for price drops. Arbitrica searches the same hotel from 195 countries at the checkout and repeats that search over time. The two differ in scope, in automation and in the kind of saving they find.

## How RoomHawk works

RoomHawk says it searches and tracks over 2 million hotel rooms. You pick a stay, RoomHawk monitors the price, and you hear about it when the rate drops. Its focus is drops over time: rates falling because demand shifts, guests cancel or a hotel releases last-minute inventory. RoomHawk's site describes it as a way to discover hotels, compare prices and monitor price drops for a chosen stay. It also advertises AI-powered auto-rebooking, though its site does not explain how that step works.

What RoomHawk does not describe is searching the same hotel from other countries. Its tracking looks at the price available to you, and catches the moment that price goes down.

## How Arbitrica works

Arbitrica is a personal AI agent that lives in your browser. It meets you at the checkout when you are about to pay, searches the same hotel from 195 countries, and finds the lowest price on Earth for it, at a different seller from the one you are on. It then prepares the booking, so you only review and pay.

It uses two families of mechanisms:

- **Geographic arbitrage.** Different countries get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions. Searching the same hotel from another country can open up lower prices you cannot get from your own country.

- **Time arbitrage.** Live arbitrage runs the search at checkout, in real time. Multiple-time arbitrage repeats the search after you book, until the free-cancellation window ends, and prepares a re-booking at the lower price. The new booking is confirmed before the original is cancelled.

Arbitrica validates that any lower price is for the identical hotel, includes all taxes and fees, and will not suddenly change before you pay.

## The geo-arbitrage gap: two different kinds of saving

A price tracker like RoomHawk is built for catching drops over time. A hotel that costs $250 today may drop to $220 next week because demand falls. A tracker catches that $30 saving.

Arbitrica finds a different kind of saving. Searched from Indonesia or Portugal, that same $250 hotel may come in lower right now, because those countries get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions. These differences exist at the same moment, and the same hotel is often 5-30% cheaper from another country.

> Chart: The same item, with the same dates and terms, is often 5–30% cheaper bought from another country or at another moment.

In a test of 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, 80% of countries showed a lower price than the traveler's own country, and the typical gap was 20-40%. Searching all 195 countries by hand is impossible in practice. Arbitrica does it at the checkout, and its multiple-time approach also covers the drops over time that RoomHawk looks for.

In one real booking, Booking.com offered $1,527.39 and Arbitrica found $1,180.27 for the identical hotel, earning the user $347.12 (23%). Research showed that people earn on average $47 per hotel booking with Arbitrica, and up to $3,000 a year.

## User experience differences

With a tracker, you set up each stay you want watched, and the system begins monitoring. Then you wait for a drop, and act on it before your cancellation deadline.

Arbitrica needs no setup per booking, because it is there at the checkout when you are about to pay. When it finds a lower price, it has already validated that the price is for the identical hotel and the same room type with all taxes and fees, and it prepares the booking so you only review and pay. In the multiple-time approach, the re-booking it prepares matches your original hotel and terms, and the new booking is confirmed before the original is cancelled.

## What to check before choosing any hotel price tracker

- **When does it work?** Before you pay, after you book, or both.

- **From how many countries does it search?** One country only catches drops over time on the price you already see.

- **Does it compare totals?** Taxes and fees decide whether a drop is real.

- **Who does the rebooking?** If it is you, set a reminder for your cancellation deadline.

- **Does it confirm the new booking first?** The original should be cancelled only after the replacement is confirmed.

## When RoomHawk works well

RoomHawk is a reasonable choice for travelers who want to catch drops over time on a stay they have chosen. Its large database of rooms gives broad coverage for price-drop monitoring.

## When Arbitrica is the better choice

For anyone who wants the lowest price on a hotel, in their own country or abroad, Arbitrica finds a larger kind of saving. The search from 195 countries reaches prices a single-country tracker never checks, and covering both the checkout and the time after booking means the lowest price can be found before you pay and again before the free-cancellation window closes. The same mechanisms also apply beyond hotels, to flights, event tickets and online shopping, and they serve companies as well as individual travelers: teams heading to trade shows and conferences, operations managers and travel managers.
