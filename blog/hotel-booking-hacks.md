## Before you search: set yourself up to pay less

### 1. Move your dates by a day and watch the pattern

**Why it works:** hotels price each night separately, and demand is not spread evenly across the week. Hotels that depend on business travelers fill up from Monday to Thursday and often drop rates at the weekend. Resort and leisure hotels usually do the opposite.

**How:** before you commit, search your exact dates, then the same length of stay shifted one day earlier and one day later. If one version is clearly lower, you have learned which pattern your destination follows. The guide to the [best time to book a hotel](https://arbitrica.com/blog/best-time-to-book-hotels.html) covers booking windows and seasons in more detail.

### 2. Check the city's event calendar first

**Why it works:** a trade show, a big match or a concert can push every hotel in a city to its highest rates for those nights. No booking trick beats arriving the week after the crowd leaves.

**How:** search the city name with your month and words like "conference", "festival" or "trade fair". If your trip is flexible and an event lands on your dates, moving by a few days is often the single biggest saving available. If you cannot move, book a refundable rate early, because event prices tend to climb as the date approaches.

### 3. Look one neighborhood over

**Why it works:** hotels price against their nearest competitors. A hotel one metro stop outside the main tourist area competes with a different set of hotels and is often priced lower for a similar standard.

**How:** on the map view, widen your search to areas with a direct transit line to where you will spend your days. Add the cost and time of getting in and out before you decide; a lower rate that adds two taxi rides a day is not a saving.

### 4. Join the hotel's own loyalty program before you search

**Why it works:** many hotel chains show a member rate to signed-in members that is lower than the public rate, and membership is usually open to anyone with an email address.

**How:** create the account before you search the chain's own site, then sign in so the member rate appears. Note the member rate and its cancellation terms so you can weigh it against every other offer for the same hotel.

## While you book: make sure the price is really the price

### 5. Compare the total, never the nightly rate

**Why it works:** resort fees, city taxes, cleaning charges and service fees are often added late in the booking flow, and some sites show prices before tax while others include it. Two offers with the same nightly rate can end with very different totals.

**How:** go as far as the final payment screen on each option you are considering and write down the full amount for the whole stay. Check the fine print for fees payable at the hotel, which some sites list separately and leave out of the total.

### 6. Pay in the hotel's local currency

**Why it works:** when a site or a card terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, it usually applies its own exchange rate, which can be worse than your card's.

**How:** choose the local currency when you are given the option, and pay with a card that does not add foreign transaction charges. For a pay-at-hotel booking, decline the conversion offer at the front desk as well.

### 7. Choose free cancellation for any trip more than a few weeks away

**Why it works:** a non-refundable rate is a bet that the price will never fall. A refundable rate costs a little more on day one but keeps your option to rebook if the hotel gets cheaper, which happens often between booking and arrival.

**How:** filter for free cancellation, then read the exact deadline. "Free cancellation until 48 hours before arrival" in the hotel's local time is very different from "until the day of booking".

### 8. Check what breakfast and extras really cost

**Why it works:** a rate with breakfast included can be a good deal or a poor one depending on what breakfast costs at the hotel and what the café next door charges.

**How:** compare the room-only rate plus the hotel's posted breakfast price against the breakfast-included rate. Do the same for parking, which some city hotels charge per night.

### 9. Price the stay as a whole and in parts

**Why it works:** some hotels discount longer stays, and some rates have minimum-stay rules that make one night of your trip far more expensive than the others.

**How:** if one night is pushing the total up, price the stay as two separate bookings, or check whether adding a night unlocks a weekly or long-stay rate. The guide to [long-stay hotel savings](https://arbitrica.com/blog/long-stay-hotel-savings.html) goes deeper on this.

### 10. Ask the hotel directly

**Why it works:** a hotel pays a commission on bookings made through third-party sites, so some hotels are happy to offer a direct guest something extra, such as breakfast, late checkout or a better room type.

**How:** once you know the best total you can get, email or call the hotel and ask what they can do for a direct booking. Compare the answer on total cost and on cancellation terms, not on the headline rate.

### 11. Search the same hotel from other countries

**Why it works:** different countries get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions. Searching the same hotel from another country can open up prices that you cannot get from your own country.

## After you book: the savings most people leave behind

### 12. Keep checking the price until free cancellation ends

**Why it works:** hotel prices keep moving after you pay. If your booking is refundable, every drop before the deadline is money you can still earn.

**How:** check the same hotel, dates and room type every few days. If the total falls by enough to matter, book the new rate first, make sure it is confirmed, then cancel the original. The full method is in the guide to how to [rebook a hotel at a lower price](https://arbitrica.com/blog/rebook-hotel-at-lower-price.html).

### 13. Put the cancellation deadline in your calendar

**Why it works:** the window for rebooking closes on a fixed date, and a refundable booking you forget about is no more useful than a non-refundable one.

**How:** add a reminder two days before the deadline, converted to your own time zone. Use it for a last price check and to cancel any booking you no longer need.

### 14. Know which price guarantees still exist

**Why it works:** a price match can pay you the difference without rebooking, but only if the program exists and your claim meets its rules.

**How:** read the current terms before relying on one. ## Popular hotel hacks that rarely help

Some tips get repeated so often that they feel true. A few are worth dropping:

- **"Always book on a Tuesday."** Hotel prices are set night by night by systems that react to demand every day. A rule that held for one city in one season does not carry over to the next.

- **"Clear your cookies or use a private window."** It can reset what a site remembers about you, but it does not change which country you are searching from, so it does not open up the inventory and local rates available in other countries.

- **"Last minute is always lower."** Unsold hotels sometimes cut rates close to arrival, but popular dates can also climb or sell out. Booking early on a refundable rate and rechecking gives you the upside without the risk.

- **"Non-refundable is the best deal."** It is the lowest price on the day you book, and only stays the best deal if the price never falls afterward.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best hack to save money on a hotel?

The biggest single difference usually comes from searching the same hotel from other countries, where the same stay can be offered at a lower price. After that, booking a refundable rate and rebooking if the price drops before the deadline is the most reliable habit.

### Is it cheaper to book a hotel directly?

Sometimes. Hotel chains often show lower member rates to signed-in members, and some hotels add extras for direct guests. Compare the total cost and the cancellation terms against every other offer for the same hotel.

### Do hotel prices go down after you book?

They often move after booking, in both directions. If your booking has free cancellation, you can book the lower rate, wait for confirmation and then cancel the original.

### Should I pay for a hotel in local currency?

Usually yes. Paying in your home currency through the site or the hotel's card terminal often applies a worse exchange rate than your card would.
