# Which booking sites guarantee the final price upfront?

Published 2026-11-10 by Arbitrica. Canonical: https://arbitrica.com/blog/which-booking-sites-guarantee-final-price-upfront.html

## Key takeaways
- No booking site guarantees a single final number for every hotel worldwide; what the first price includes depends on the site and on the law where you book.
- In the US, the FTC rule in force since May 12, 2025 requires hotels and booking sites to show the total with all mandatory fees, such as resort fees, upfront; government taxes may be shown separately.
- In the UK, mandatory fees have had to be in the headline price since April 6, 2025.
- Expedia says the total on its search page includes all taxes and fees; Booking labels each price "Includes taxes and charges" or "Additional charges may apply" (checked on November 10, 2026).
- A guaranteed total is not the lowest total: research across 1,000 hotels found a lower price in about 80% of countries, with a typical gap of 20-40%.

## Short answer

No booking site guarantees one final number for every hotel in every country. In the US, an FTC rule in force since May 12, 2025 makes hotels and booking sites show the total price with every mandatory fee upfront, although government taxes can still be shown separately, and in the UK mandatory fees have had to be in the headline price since April 6, 2025. Expedia says the total on its search page includes all taxes and fees, and Booking labels each price with what it includes, so the safest final number is the order total on the last page plus anything listed as payable at the hotel.

## What a final price has to include

A hotel price has up to five parts, and a site can show any combination of them as the first number you see:

- **The room rate** for your dates, room type and meal plan.
- **Mandatory hotel fees** such as resort, destination, cleaning or service fees.
- **Taxes** such as VAT, sales or occupancy tax.
- **Charges paid at the hotel**, often a city or tourist tax collected on arrival.
- **Currency conversion**, when the hotel charges in its own currency and you pay in yours.

"Final price upfront" only means something when all five are in the first number, or when the site tells you clearly which are not.

## What the rules require (checked on November 10, 2026)

| Where | Rule | What it means for hotel prices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| United States | FTC Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees, in force since May 12, 2025 | Hotels, vacation rental platforms and live-event ticket sellers must show the total price with all mandatory fees at the start, more prominently than other prices. Government taxes, shipping and optional extras can be left out, but must be disclosed before you pay. |
| United Kingdom | Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act, drip pricing ban from April 6, 2025 | All mandatory fees, such as admin or booking fees, must be in the headline price. Optional extras are not covered. |
| European Union | EU consumer law, enforced by national authorities through the CPC network | Booking (2019) and Expedia Group (2020) committed to change how they present offers, discounts and prices; Airbnb committed to show the total price with all mandatory charges and fees. |

The US rule is the most specific, and it covers live-event tickets for the same reason: a fee added at the last step made prices impossible to judge. ## What can still change the total

- **City and tourist taxes paid on arrival.** Many European cities collect them at the hotel, so they can sit outside the online total even when it is labelled as including taxes.
- **Currency.** If the hotel charges in its own currency, the amount on your card depends on the exchange rate on the day, plus any card fee.
- **Pay-at-hotel rates.** The price is set when you book, but the charge happens later, in the hotel's currency.
- **Optional extras.** Breakfast, parking or late check-out are outside every rule above.
- **Deposits.** A security deposit is not a fee, but it changes what leaves your account.

## How to check the real total before you pay

1. **Go to the last page before payment** The order total there is the number that counts, not the search result.
2. **Read every line under the total** Look for "not included", "pay at the property", "city tax" or "additional charges may apply".
3. **Check the currency** Note which currency you will be charged in and when.
4. **Match the conditions** When you weigh two offers, use the same room type, meal plan and cancellation policy, or the totals mean nothing.
5. **Book a rate with free cancellation if you can** If a lower total appears later, you can [rebook at the lower price](https://arbitrica.com/blog/rebook-hotel-at-lower-price.html) and cancel the original once the new booking is confirmed.

## A final price is not the lowest price

Upfront pricing tells you what a site will charge. It says nothing about what the same hotel costs on another site or from another country, where inventory, sales channels, local rates and promotions differ, and dynamic pricing moves each of them separately. Research across 1,000 hotels demonstrated a lower price in about 80% of countries, with a typical gap of 20-40%. In [Arbitrica's own price checks](https://arbitrica.com/blog/hotel-prices-by-country-data.html), a 4-night stay at White House Hotel Istanbul cost $1,066 searched from the UK and $708 searched from India.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Booking.com show the total price upfront?
Booking labels each price with what it includes, for example "Includes taxes and charges" or "Additional charges may apply", and lists excluded charges separately. Charges paid at the property, such as some city taxes, can still be outside the online total, so read the lines under the total on the last page.

### Does Expedia include taxes and fees in the price?
Expedia says the total on its search page includes all taxes and fees, marked with an "Upfront pricing" note (Expedia newsroom, April 23, 2025, checked on November 10, 2026). Charges the hotel collects in person and currency conversion can still change what you pay.

### Are resort fees included in US hotel prices now?
They should be. Since May 12, 2025 the FTC fees rule requires hotels and booking sites to include all mandatory fees, such as resort fees, in the total price shown upfront. Government taxes can be shown separately, as long as they are disclosed before you pay.

### Is city tax included in the hotel price?
Not always. Many cities collect a tourist or city tax at the hotel, so it can appear as "pay at the property" rather than in the online total. Check the price breakdown on the last page before payment.

### Can the price change after I book?
The price you booked is fixed, but the amount charged can move if you pay later in the hotel's currency. The price for the same stay can also fall after you book; with a free-cancellation rate you can rebook at the lower price and cancel the original once the new booking is confirmed.
