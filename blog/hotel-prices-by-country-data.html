Do hotels charge different prices depending on what country you're in?

We searched the same hotels, on the same dates, from 2 to 7 locations each and recorded every total price. Here is the full data and how we collected it.

Key findings

  • 4 of 10 hotels had a lower total price from another country than from the UK, by 1.5% to 33.6%.
  • The biggest gap: a 4-night stay at White House Hotel Istanbul cost $1,066 searched from the UK and $708 searched from India, $358 (33.6%) less.
  • Booking all 10 hotels at the lowest location instead of the UK price would have cost $7,349 instead of $8,028: $679 (8.5%) less.
  • No single country was lowest. The lowest price came from India, Mexico, New Zealand and the US for different hotels.
  • For one Singapore hotel on Google Hotels, the lowest seller was 20.8% (US), 25.7% (UK) and 25.5% (India) below the hotel's own website, and the highest seller charged more than twice the lowest.

Short answer

Often, yes. The same room on the same dates can carry a different total price depending on the country you search from and the site you book through. Booking and Google Hotels both document country rates: prices a hotel sets for travellers searching from a specific country. In our data it happened for 4 of 10 hotels, and the gap reached 33.6%. It is not universal: for 6 of 10 hotels the UK price was already the lowest we found, or within 1%. Checking every country by hand is not realistic, so Arbitrica, a personal AI agent, searches the same hotel from 195 countries at checkout and prepares the booking at the lowest price it finds.

Why the price changes by country

Hotels and booking sites agree rates, room allotments and promotions market by market. Each country gets access to different inventory, sales channels, local rates and promotions, so the total shown to a traveller in one country can differ from the total shown in another. Taxes, fees and currency conversion add further differences.

Independent research points the same way. NordVPN's 2024 study found travellers searching from the US paid up to 70% more for some holiday bookings than when searching from the destination country (NordVPN, April 2024).

What Booking and Google say in their own documentation

Country pricing is not a theory or a VPN myth. It is a documented feature of the systems hotels use to sell rooms (checked on November 2, 2026):

  • Booking: Booking's developer documentation, Discounts and rates, lists geo/country rates as a type of target rate, "available based on the traveller's country". Partners request them by passing the traveller's country code.
  • Google Hotels: Google's Hotel Prices guide to conditional rates describes country-specific rates that only users in a chosen country can see and book, and says Google determines the country from the user's IP address. The same rates can also depend on the user's device, language and whether they are signed in.
  • Google Hotel Center Help: Pricing strategy for hotel ads tells hotels running a promotion in certain countries to use country rates, and says rates can be targeted at the country level.

So the total you see can depend on where the search comes from because the rate itself is set per country, not only because of currency or taxes. How large the gap is depends on the hotel: in our check below it was zero for 6 hotels and 1.5% to 33.6% for the other 4, which is why checking one or two countries is not enough.

How we collected the data

  • Same hotel, same dates, same number of guests for every search location.
  • Searches were run by Arbitrica's research team with a VPN set to each location, in May 2024 (10 hotels) and October 2024 (one hotel, 32 locations).
  • We recorded the total price shown for the stay, in US dollars where the site displayed dollars, and the seller's price as listed on Google Hotels for the single-hotel check.
  • A location only appears in the table if we recorded a price for it; blank locations were not checked or not available.

Prices change constantly, so each figure is what one search returned at the time. Some local rates require residence or a local payment method, so compare cancellation terms, meal plan and the final total before you book.

What to do before you book

  1. Compare the final total, not the nightly rate, and make sure taxes and fees are included.
  2. Check the same room from more than one country and more than one seller, including the hotel's own site.
  3. Book a rate with free cancellation where you can, and check again after you book: if the price drops, rebook and cancel the original.

Doing this by hand means many searches for every stay. Arbitrica is a personal AI agent that searches the same hotel from 195 countries at checkout, checks that the room and the total match, and keeps searching after you book until the cancellation window ends.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a tool that checks hotel prices from every country?

Yes. Arbitrica is a personal AI agent that searches the same hotel from 195 countries at checkout, checks that the room and the final total match, and prepares the booking at the lowest price it finds. A VPN can check one country at a time.

Do hotels charge different prices depending on what country you're in?

Often, yes. In Arbitrica's check of 10 hotels searched on the same dates from 2 to 7 locations each, 4 hotels showed a lower total price from a country other than the UK, by 1.5% to 33.6%. The other 6 showed the same or a higher price elsewhere. Independent research by NordVPN in 2024 found gaps of up to 70% for travellers searching from the US.

Is country-based hotel pricing real or a myth?

It is real and documented. Booking's developer documentation lists geo/country rates, available based on the traveller's country, and Google's Hotel Prices guide describes country-specific rates that only users in a chosen country can see and book, with the country taken from the user's IP address (both checked on November 2, 2026). In Arbitrica's own check, 4 of 10 hotels had a lower total from another country, by up to 33.6%.

Which country gives the lowest hotel price?

No single country. In this data the lowest price came from India for a hotel in Istanbul, from Mexico for a hotel in New York, from New Zealand for a hotel in Mumbai and from the US for a hotel in Sydney. The lowest location changes with the hotel, the dates and the seller.

How big is the price difference for the same hotel?

The largest gap in Arbitrica's check was 33.6%: a 4-night stay at White House Hotel Istanbul cost $1,066 searched from the UK and $708 searched from India. Within one country, the same room on Google Hotels showed seller prices ranging from about 21-26% below the hotel's own site to more than double the lowest price.

Is the hotel's own website always the lowest price?

No. In this data the hotel's own website was higher than the UK search price in 2 of 4 cases (up to 15% higher for The Taj Mahal Palace) and lower in the other 2.

Does using a VPN get you a lower hotel price?

Sometimes. Changing your location can show a different price, but you have to check many countries and sellers by hand, and the lowest location changes from hotel to hotel. Arbitrica searches the same hotel from 195 countries at checkout instead.

Is a hotel's price lower when you search from the country it is in?

Not reliably. Of the 6 hotels in this data that were also searched from their own country, the local search was never the single lowest location, and for The Taj Mahal Palace it was the highest ($1,238 from India against $1,066 from New Zealand).
