Why Booking.com prices differ by country

The same hotel on the same dates can cost less searched from another country. Here is why, how big the gap is, and how to book it safely.

Booking.com is the world's largest hotel booking platform, with millions of properties listed. What most travelers do not realize is that the same hotel, on the same dates, can cost less when it is searched from another country. A stay at The Savoy, London, for example, can be priced differently depending on the country it is searched from. Knowing why makes it much easier to stop overpaying.

How prices for the same hotel differ by country

A hotel sells through many channels at once: Booking.com, other booking sites, wholesalers, local travel agencies and its own website. Many of those agreements are made market by market. Different countries therefore get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions, and searching the same hotel from another country can open up prices you cannot get from your own country.

The main reasons a search from another country can come back lower:

  • Market rates. Hotels and their partners agree rates for specific markets.
  • Local and domestic rates. Some rates are meant for travelers in one country.
  • Promotions. Seasonal sales, loyalty deals and campaigns often run in only some countries. A deal visible in Germany may not appear in the United States.
  • Local sellers. Some booking sites and agencies sell in only a few countries, with their own allotments.
  • How totals are shown. Taxes and fees are included up front in some countries and added at the end in others, so always compare the final total.

How big are the differences?

For the same hotel and dates, the price found from the right country is often 5-30% below the price offered from your own country. On a long or expensive stay that adds up fast.

In a test of 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, 80% of countries showed a lower price than the traveler's own country, and the typical gap was 20-40%. Independent research found the same pattern: a 2024 NordVPN study found that travel bookings made through US websites came out up to 70% higher than through websites elsewhere (its largest gap was a car rental). In one case, a seven-night stay in a triple room at a Milan hotel for a family of three cost about $1,216 searched from the US and $1,012 searched from Italy.

A real example: Booking.com offered one hotel stay for $1,527.39. Arbitrica found the identical hotel and dates for $1,180.27, with all taxes and fees included. That earned the user $347.12, or 23%.

Why hotels and booking sites work this way

Demand and purchasing power vary a lot between countries. Selling through partners and promotions that target each market lets hotels fill more of their inventory without lowering their rate everywhere. It is standard practice across the industry, not just at Booking.com: Expedia, Hotels.com, Agoda and Trip.com all sell the same hotels through many markets and channels.

There is also a timing layer on top. Hotel revenue-management systems move rates as occupancy and demand change, so the gap between countries is not fixed either. A country that is lowest for a hotel this week may not be lowest next week. For more on that, see how hotel dynamic pricing works.

Can you get the lower prices?

Often, yes. Many of these are real, bookable rates. Check the conditions before you pay: some local rates are limited to residents of a country, and the hotel can ask for proof at check-in.

The hard part is knowing which country has the lowest price for a specific hotel. It changes with demand, promotions and inventory. A hotel that is lowest when searched from Thailand today may be lowest from Brazil tomorrow. What to compare before you book from another country

A lower number is only a saving when everything else matches. Before you pay, line up these details for both offers:

DetailWhy it matters
Hotel name and addressSimilar names can belong to different properties.
Dates and guestsA change of one night or one guest resets the price.
Cancellation policyA non-refundable rate is often lower, but removes your option to rebook.
Meal planBreakfast included on one offer and not the other can explain the gap.
Final totalTaxes, city tax and resort fees decide the real price.
Currency and card feesA foreign-currency payment can add a card fee to the total.
Rate conditionsResidents-only rates can be refused at check-in.
Tip

If your card offers to charge you in your own currency at checkout, compare that amount with the local-currency total. The conversion rate offered at checkout is often worse than your card's own rate.

Common myths about booking from another country

  • "One country is always cheapest." The lowest country changes with the hotel, the dates, promotions and inventory. A rule that worked for one trip rarely holds for the next.
  • "Switching my connection reprices the hotel." The difference comes from the inventory, sellers, local rates and promotions another country has access to, not from a booking site changing the same listing for you.
  • "A lower headline rate means a lower price." Taxes and fees are shown differently in different countries. Only the final total counts.
  • "Rates from another country are not real." Many are ordinary bookable rates with normal confirmations. The exception is a rate limited to residents, which the hotel can refuse if you do not qualify.

Keeping these in mind saves time: you stop chasing a magic country and start comparing like for like.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the same hotel cost less from another country?

Hotels and their partners agree rates, allotments and promotions market by market, so each country gets access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions. Some of those are lower than what you can get from your own country.

Is it legal to book a hotel at another country's price?

Booking a published rate is an ordinary purchase. Check the rate conditions, because some local rates are limited to residents and the hotel can ask for proof at check-in.

Which country is cheapest for Booking.com hotels?

No single country is always cheapest. The lowest country changes with the hotel, the dates, promotions and inventory, which is why checking many countries matters.

Does this only happen on Booking.com?

No. Expedia, Hotels.com, Agoda and Trip.com also sell the same hotels through many markets and channels, so prices for the same hotel differ by country there too.
