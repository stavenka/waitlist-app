You book a hotel, feel good about the price and move on to planning the rest of the trip. A week later you look at the same hotel again and the price has dropped by $40 a night. It happens often, and it is not random. Once you understand why hotel prices fall after booking, you can turn those drops into money earned.

## Short answer

Nothing happens automatically. Booking and Expedia do not refund the difference when their own price for your stay drops later. If your rate has free cancellation, you can book the same hotel again at the lower price, wait for the confirmation and cancel the original before the deadline; with a non-refundable rate you usually cannot capture the drop. Alert tools such as Rate Ranger and HotelMonitor tell you when the price falls. ## How often do hotel prices drop after booking?

Hotel prices change frequently between the day you book and the day you check in. How often a specific hotel gets cheaper depends on the destination, the season and how far ahead you book, but some patterns are well known:

- Bookings made well in advance leave more time for the price to move, in either direction.

- Hotels in competitive cities with many nearby alternatives adjust prices more often.

- Shoulder seasons, just before or after peak periods, tend to be the most volatile.

## Why hotel prices drop after you book

Hotel pricing is dynamic. Revenue management systems adjust rates based on occupancy forecasts, competitor prices, day-of-week patterns and demand signals from searches and bookings. The guide to [how hotel dynamic pricing works](https://arbitrica.com/blog/how-hotel-dynamic-pricing-works.html) goes deeper into these systems.

When a hotel is filling more slowly than forecast, the system lowers prices to stimulate demand. When a group cancels a large block of rooms, prices can drop to fill the sudden inventory. When a competing hotel cuts its rate, the system may match it.

These adjustments can happen daily or even several times a day. The price you paid was right for that moment, but the market kept moving.

## The free cancellation opportunity

Many hotel bookings come with free cancellation until a day or two before check-in. That creates a simple opportunity: if the price for the identical hotel drops after you book, you can make a new booking at the lower price and cancel the original. You stay at the same hotel on the same dates.

Make the new booking first and cancel the original only once the new one is confirmed. Before you cancel, check that the new booking is for the same hotel and room type, that the new price includes all taxes and fees, and that cancelling the original booking really is free.

The catch is the effort. Checking a booking once a day for 30 days means 30 manual searches for a single hotel, and more for every additional trip.

## Which bookings can be rebooked

Whether a drop is useful to you depends on the terms of the booking you already hold.

| Booking type | Can you rebook at a lower price? | What to watch |

| Free cancellation, pay at the hotel | Yes, until the cancellation deadline | Deadlines are often set in the hotel's local time |

| Free cancellation, prepaid | Yes, until the cancellation deadline | The refund can take a few days to reach your card, so both charges may show for a while |

| Partly refundable | Only if the drop is bigger than the cancellation fee | Subtract the fee before you count the saving |

| Non-refundable | Usually no | The lower headline price at booking removes your chance to benefit later |

This is why a refundable rate that costs a little more on day one is often the better deal for trips more than a few weeks away.

None of the big booking sites lowers your price on its own when the same site drops its price after you book. Each has a different rule, and the rules below were checked on each company's own site on November 3, 2026.

## How to rebook a hotel at a lower price

- **Note your booking details**Write down the hotel, dates, guests, room type, meal plan, total paid and the exact cancellation deadline.

- **Search the identical hotel again**Check the same dates and terms every few days, and more often close to the deadline.

- **Compare the final total**Compare totals with all taxes and fees included, not the nightly rate.

- **Book the new rate first**Wait for the confirmation of the new booking before you touch the old one.

- **Cancel the original**Cancel before the deadline and keep the cancellation confirmation.

## Mistakes that wipe out a hotel price drop

Most failed rebookings come from a small number of avoidable slips:

- **Cancelling first.** If you cancel before the new booking is confirmed, the lower rate can disappear while you are paying, and you may lose the hotel altogether on busy dates.

- **Comparing different rooms.** A lower price for a smaller room type, or a rate without breakfast, is not the same booking.

- **Missing a fee.** Resort fees, city taxes and payment fees can be added late in the booking flow. A drop in the nightly rate that is eaten by a new fee is not a drop.

- **Misreading the deadline.** Cancellation deadlines are often set in the hotel's local time, which can be hours earlier than you expect.

- **Switching to non-refundable.** Rebooking into a non-refundable rate ends your chance to benefit from the next drop. Keep the new booking flexible if you can.

## Price drops over time and across countries

A price drop over time is one source of savings. Another is geographic: the same hotel is often 5-30% cheaper when searched from another country, because different countries get access to different inventory, channels, local rates and promotions. The two can add up. A hotel that falls from $300 to $260 a night over a few weeks may at the same time be available for less when searched from another country.

## How do I get an alert when my booked hotel price drops?

You do not have to check by hand. These are the alerts available as of November 2026, each checked against its own announcement or listing (Rate Ranger and HotelMonitor on November 3, 2026):

- **Google Hotels:** since April 17, 2026, signed-in users can turn on price tracking for an individual hotel and specific dates in Google Search or on Google Hotels, and get an email when the rate changes significantly. It works in English and Spanish.

- **RatePunk:** its extension and app notify you if it notices a price drop for a hotel you booked, so you can cancel and rebook yourself.

- **Repriced and OneAir:** they watch bookings after you reserve, and their own announcements say they rebook or secure credit when the price drops.

- **Rate Ranger:** it watches a booking you already made on Booking, Agoda, Expedia, Hotels.com, Trip.com, KAYAK or direct with the hotel, checks every few days and more often near check-in, stops 72 hours before check-in and emails you a link to the lower rate. You book it and cancel the old booking yourself.

- **HotelMonitor:** a UK service that re-checks Booking, Expedia, Hotels.com, Agoda, Kayak and Trip.com up to every 4 hours for a stay you booked or are considering, and emails you when it finds a lower price. You decide whether to rebook.

- **Booking's price match:** not an alert, but if you find the same stay for less on another public site you can [claim the difference](https://arbitrica.com/blog/booking-com-price-match.html), which guides report is possible until 24 hours before check-in.
