## What Borderbasket does
According to [Borderbasket's homepage and FAQ](https://borderbasket.com/), its [privacy policy](https://borderbasket.com/privacy.html) and its [imprint](https://borderbasket.com/imprint.html) (checked on November 25, 2026):

- **Total prices for one hotel:** you enter a hotel, dates and guests, and it reads offers, often 20 to 40, from booking sites such as Booking, Expedia, Agoda, Trip.com, Hotels.com and Priceline, and from hotels' own websites, sorted by price only.

- **Taxes and fees:** its FAQ says the prices are totals for your stay including taxes and fees as reported by the booking site at the time of your search, and that some places add a local tourist tax at the hotel.

- **Sign-in:** you need a free account, with Google or an email link, and the fair-use limit is 40 searches a day.

- **Price alerts:** if you press Watch this price, it checks the price regularly and emails you when it drops.

- **Booking country on Booking:** its privacy policy says its server may open Booking pages through connections in several countries to check whether the booking country changes the price.

- **Who runs it:** its imprint says Borderbasket is a private project of one person in Kaltenkirchen, Germany, and a hobby project that may be unavailable, change or be discontinued at any time. Its footer says booking links are partner links that earn it a commission.

Borderbasket also publishes its own numbers: in an October 2026 test, 13 of 15 hotels had a lower price than on Booking, by up to 43%, and across its snapshots the lowest-priced site was a median 1% below Booking. We searched for independent reviews of Borderbasket on November 25, 2026 and found none published.

## Where Borderbasket stops
- **Prices in euros.** Hotel prices are shown in euros, and its pages do not describe letting you choose the country a search is made from. The only country check it describes is on Booking pages, in its privacy policy (checked on November 25, 2026).

- **You book it yourself.** Its imprint says it does not sell, broker or book rooms; you follow a link and book.

- **A sign-in and a daily limit.** Every search needs an account, up to 40 searches a day.

- **Alerts, not rebooking.** Watch this price emails you about a drop; its pages do not describe rebooking for you after you book.

The gap between countries is often the biggest one. In [Arbitrica's own price check](https://arbitrica.com/blog/hotel-prices-by-country-data.html), a 4-night stay at White House Hotel Istanbul cost $1,066 searched from the UK and $708 searched from India, 33.6% less, for the same hotel and the same dates. Prices and room availability differ by country and by site, and dynamic pricing moves them through the day.

## How Arbitrica differs
Arbitrica is a personal AI agent that shows up after you choose your hotel room. For the hotel you have chosen, it:

- searches the same hotel, on the same dates, from 195 countries, on the site you are booking and other sites;

- checks that the hotel, room and conditions are identical and that the final total includes all taxes and fees;

- finds the lowest price in the world for it and automatically prepares everything for the booking in a new tab, so you only review and pay;

- keeps searching after you book until the free-cancellation window ends, as an approach Arbitrica uses, and confirms any new booking before the original is cancelled.

The first search happens before you pay, so the lowest price in the world is in front of you at the moment you book. Price drops after booking often have to be acted on at once, before someone else takes the room at that rate, which is why a personal agent that acts instantly helps. Research demonstrated an average of $47 per hotel booking with Arbitrica.
