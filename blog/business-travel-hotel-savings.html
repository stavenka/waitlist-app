Hotels are one of the largest controllable costs in a business travel program, and one of the least examined. Companies negotiate rates, set nightly caps and steer travelers toward preferred channels, yet the same hotel on the same dates is often 5-30% cheaper when it is searched from another country. That gap sits outside most travel policies, and it can be captured without changing which hotel a traveler stays in.

This article is for the people who carry hotel spend inside a company: travel managers who run the program, operations managers who book travel for staff, and teams heading to trade shows and conferences. Arbitrica serves companies as well as individual travelers, and the approach below works for both.

The scale of business travel hotel spend

The Global Business Travel Association (GBTA) projected global business travel spending of about $1.48 trillion in 2024, and lodging is one of the biggest line items in that total. For a single company the numbers add up quickly. Take a company with 150 traveling employees who each spend 40 nights a year in hotels at an average of $220 a night: that is 6,000 hotel nights and $1.32 million in annual hotel spend.

At the low end of the typical spread, a price 5% lower for the identical hotel is worth $66,000 a year to that company. At 15%, it is worth $198,000.

Why negotiated corporate hotel rates leave money on the table

Travel management companies (TMCs) and corporate travel teams negotiate rates with hotel chains, usually as a discount off the best available rate. Those agreements are valuable, but they cover only one part of hotel pricing. A negotiated rate is set for one market, normally the company's own country, and it says nothing about the prices available for the same hotel from other countries.

Different countries get access to different inventory, distribution channels, local rates and promotions. Searching the same hotel from another country can therefore open up prices that are not available from your own country, sometimes below a negotiated corporate rate. In a test of 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, 80% of countries showed a lower price than the traveler's own country.

An illustrative example

Say a London hotel shows a public rate of $250 a night from the United States and your negotiated corporate rate is $220. That looks like a solid 12% discount. If the same hotel, for the same dates and conditions, comes up at $185 a night when searched from another country, the corporate rate is no longer the best price available to your traveler.

Why doing this by hand does not scale

A traveler with a VPN can switch to another country and search again, one country at a time. Doing that for 195 countries on every trip, while checking that each result is the identical hotel with all taxes and fees included, is impossible in practice. Travel managers cannot ask employees to spend hours on it, and spot checks from three or four countries miss most of the spread.

How Arbitrica works for companies

Arbitrica is a personal AI agent that lives in your browser. When anyone in the company, a traveler or the person booking for them, reaches the checkout for a hotel, Arbitrica searches the same hotel from 195 countries and finds the lowest price on Earth for it, at a different seller from the one they are on. It validates that the lower price is for the identical hotel and includes all taxes and fees, checks that the price will not suddenly change, and then prepares the booking so the person booking only reviews and pays.

Two mechanisms work together:

  • Geographic arbitrage at checkout (live arbitrage): the same hotel is searched from 195 countries in real time, before the traveler pays.
  • Multiple-time arbitrage: hotel prices keep moving after booking. Arbitrica's approach repeats the search after the booking is made, until the free-cancellation window ends, and prepares a re-booking at the lower price. The new booking is confirmed before the original reservation is cancelled.

The same mechanisms apply beyond hotels. Flights, car rentals, event tickets and other purchases with a cancellation window can be searched from other countries and checked again over time.

Who inside a company gets the most from it

Travel managers

Travel managers own the hotel line in the budget and answer for it. Negotiated rates and caps set a ceiling, but they do not tell you whether the same hotel was available for less from another country on the day it was booked. Arbitrica searches every hotel at the checkout from 195 countries, so the price the company pays is the lowest it could find for the identical hotel, with all taxes and fees included. Operations managers

Operations managers and office managers often book hotels for colleagues, crews or visiting staff, many trips a month and usually under time pressure. Checking other countries by hand is out of the question at that volume. With Arbitrica, the search happens when they reach the checkout, and the lower-priced booking is prepared for them to review and pay. See travel costs for operations managers.

Teams traveling to trade shows and conferences

Trade shows and conferences are where hotel spend concentrates. A company sends several people to the same city on the same dates, when demand near the venue is high and prices tend to rise. That is when searching the same hotel from 195 countries matters most: each booking is checked at the checkout, not only the first one. Event hotels are also often booked weeks or months ahead with free cancellation, which leaves a long window for the multiple-time approach to repeat the search before the trip. Travel policy and duty of care

Arbitrica looks for the identical hotel the traveler already chose. The dates, room type and cancellation terms stay the same, so the traveler stays where the policy says they should and duty of care is unaffected. The only thing that changes is the price.

Travel managers should still confirm how a re-booked reservation is recorded in their booking tool and expense process, and whether bookings made outside a preferred channel count toward volume commitments negotiated with a hotel chain.

Practical points for travel and operations managers

  • Arbitrica looks for the identical hotel, dates and terms the traveler chose, so no change of hotel is needed.
  • A rate with free cancellation keeps the window open for the search to be repeated after booking.
  • Re-bookings are prepared for review, and the new booking is confirmed before the original is cancelled, so the traveler is never left without a hotel.
  • Comparisons always use the final total with taxes and fees, which is also the number finance sees on the expense report.
