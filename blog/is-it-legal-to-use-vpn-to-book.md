# Is it legal to use a VPN to book hotels or flights?

Updated 2026-11-10 by Arbitrica. Canonical: https://arbitrica.com/blog/is-it-legal-to-use-vpn-to-book.html

## Key takeaways
- VPNs are legal in most countries, including the US, Canada, the UK and most of Europe and Latin America.
- They are banned in Belarus, Oman (for personal use), Turkmenistan, North Korea and Myanmar, and limited to approved providers in China, Russia and Iran.
- Legal is not the same as allowed: a company can enforce its own country rules, as Google did in 2024 when it cancelled YouTube Premium plans bought through foreign VPNs.
- Resident-only hotel rates and card checks matter more than the law. Arbitrica searches 195 countries for you and you pay through the seller's normal checkout.

## Short answer
In most countries, using a VPN to search for and book a hotel or flight is legal. VPNs are legal in the US, Canada, the UK and most of Europe and Latin America ([NordVPN country guide, updated September 3, 2026](https://nordvpn.com/blog/are-vpns-legal/)). What can cause trouble is not the law but the booking: some rates are only for residents, some sites enforce their own country rules, and a card from one country with an address in another can trigger checks.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the rules where you live and the conditions of the rate you book.

## Legal vs allowed by the site
Companies can still enforce their own country rules. In June 2024 Google emailed YouTube Premium members who had signed up through a foreign VPN: "We believe that you did not enter your actual country of residence when you signed up," and asked them to resubscribe or lose their benefits ([The Star, June 23, 2024](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2024/06/23/youtube-we-will-cancel-premium-subscriptions-bought-via-foreign-vpn)).

Hotels can do the same with rates meant for residents. A Phocuswright analyst told USA Today that some deals may require a home address or driving licence from that country ([USA Today test, January 2026](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/articles/tried-using-vpn-score-cheaper-100134258.html)), and hotel fraud tools score bookings higher risk when the IP country and billing country differ ([Greip, April 2026](https://greip.io/blog/The-Hoteliers-Playbook-for-Fighting-Booking-Fraud-Using-IP-Geolocation-to-Stop-Rate-Abuse-and-Fake-Reviews-364)).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it legal to use a VPN to book flights?
In most countries, yes. VPNs are legal in the US, Canada, the UK and most of Europe and Latin America. They are banned or restricted in a few countries such as Belarus, Turkmenistan, North Korea, China, Russia and Iran.

### Is it legal to use a VPN to book a hotel?
In most countries, yes. What matters more is the rate: some hotel rates are only for residents and may require a local address or ID.

### Can I get in trouble for using a VPN to book?
Legal trouble is unlikely where VPNs are legal. A site can still apply its own terms or rate conditions, for example cancelling a resident-only rate if you do not qualify.

### Is it illegal to use a VPN in the UK or US?
No. VPNs are legal in both, according to NordVPN's country guide updated September 2026.

### Can I find prices from other countries without a VPN?
Yes. Arbitrica searches the same hotel from 195 countries at the checkout and prepares the booking at the lowest price it finds, so you do not need to change your location.
