Amazon prices in different countries: why the same product costs more or less

Different stores, sellers, taxes and promotions mean one product has many prices. Here is what to check before you buy from another country.

Look up the same product on Amazon in two different countries and you will often see two different prices. Sometimes the gap is small and disappears once shipping is added. Sometimes the same model, from the same manufacturer, costs noticeably less in another country or from another seller. Knowing why that happens, and what to check before you buy, is the difference between earning a real saving and paying for a surprise at customs.

Why Amazon prices differ between countries

Amazon runs separate stores for many countries, such as amazon.com in the US, amazon.co.uk in the UK, amazon.de in Germany and amazon.co.jp in Japan. Each store has its own catalog, its own mix of sellers, its own stock and its own promotions. The same product can sit in all of them at different prices for several reasons:

  • Different sellers. On any Amazon store, a product page can be served by Amazon itself or by third-party sellers. Who holds stock in each country, and how much they want to clear it, varies.
  • Local pricing and promotions. Manufacturers and retailers set prices per market, and sales events, coupons and clearance happen at different times in different countries.
  • Taxes in the displayed price. In the UK and the EU, prices shown to consumers include VAT. In the US, sales tax is usually added at checkout. A price that looks higher may simply include tax that another store adds later.
  • Currency. Exchange rates move, while listed prices often stay put for a while, so one market can drift cheaper or more expensive relative to another.
  • Local competition. A product that is heavily discounted by competing retailers in one country tends to be cheaper on Amazon there too.

None of this is unusual. It is how prices work when the same product is sold through many channels in many markets. The result is that "the price" of a product is really a range, and where you buy from decides where in that range you land.

Same product, different price, different seller

Price differences are not only between countries. Within a single store, the same listing can have several offers: new from Amazon, new from a third-party seller, renewed, or used. Other retailers may sell the identical model too. When people search for "same product different price different country", the cheapest option often turns out to be a different seller in another market rather than the store they started on.

That is useful, but it is also where mistakes happen. A lower price from an unfamiliar seller is only a good deal if the item is identical and the seller is reliable.

What to check before you buy from another country

Before you buy, go through each of these. Skipping one is how a saving on the listed price turns into a loss.

Identical model number

Manufacturers often sell regional variants. A laptop may have a different keyboard layout, a camera may have a different kit lens, a phone may support different network bands, and an appliance may have a different plug or voltage. Match the exact model number or part number, not just the product name and photo.

The seller

Check who sells and ships the item, the seller's ratings and how long it has been selling. "Sold by" and "Ships from" can be different businesses, and that affects returns and support.

Warranty

Some manufacturer warranties are international; others only apply in the country or region of purchase. Check the manufacturer's warranty terms for the exact model before buying abroad, especially for electronics and appliances.

Shipping and delivery time

International shipping can be expensive and slow, and not every product ships to every country. Check whether the seller ships to your address and add the shipping cost to the total.

Import duties and taxes

Buying across borders can trigger import duties, VAT or sales tax, and carrier handling fees. On some international orders Amazon collects an estimated amount at checkout to cover these. Rules change, too: the United States suspended its duty-free de minimis treatment for low-value imports from all countries from August 29, 2025. Check current rules for your country before you order.

Returns

A return to another country can cost more than the saving. Check the return window, who pays return shipping and whether the seller accepts international returns.

Tip

Compare one number only: the final total delivered to your door, in your currency, with shipping, duties, taxes and fees included. A listed price is not a delivered price.

When buying from another country makes sense

Cross-border buying tends to work best for items that are easy to verify and easy to ship: branded electronics with a clear model number and an international warranty, books, toys, cosmetics, tools and accessories. It works less well for very heavy or bulky items, for products with strict regional differences, and for anything you may need to return. The guide to buying online cheaper from another country walks through more categories and examples of what to look for.

The same product is often 5-30% cheaper elsewhere, but the real question is always the delivered total. A modest gap on an expensive item is usually worth more than a big percentage on a low-priced one.

Why checking by hand rarely finds the best price

Checking two or three Amazon stores is manageable. Checking every country store, every seller and every other retailer that stocks the identical model is not. Each one means a new site, a new currency, a new set of shipping and tax rules, and a new seller to vet. Some offers are only shown when you search from that country, which means a VPN and one country at a time. Most people stop after a couple of tries, which is why the lowest price usually goes unfound. This is geographic price arbitrage, explained in more detail in what is geographic price arbitrage.

Bottom line

Amazon prices differ between countries because each store has its own sellers, stock, promotions, taxes and currency. That creates real opportunities, as long as you match the exact model, vet the seller, check the warranty and returns, and compare the delivered total with every duty and fee included. Frequently asked questions

Why are Amazon prices different in different countries?

Each Amazon store has its own sellers, stock, local promotions, tax rules and currency. The same product can therefore be listed at different prices in different countries.

Is it cheaper to buy from Amazon in another country?

Sometimes. It depends on the delivered total after shipping, import duties, taxes and fees, and on whether the model, warranty and return terms suit you.

Do I pay import duties when buying from Amazon abroad?

You may. On some international orders Amazon collects an estimated amount at checkout; otherwise duties and taxes can be charged on delivery. Check the current rules for your country.

How do I make sure it is the same product?

Match the exact model or part number, including any region code, and check accessories, plugs, language and warranty, rather than relying on the product name or photo.
