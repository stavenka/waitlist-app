Is Hopper Price Freeze worth it for hotels?

A Price Freeze protects you from the price going up, by up to $100, if you book through Hopper. It does not look for a lower price somewhere else. Here is what it covers and when it is worth the deposit.

Key takeaways

  • Hopper's June 16, 2021 launch post says you can freeze the rate at a specific hotel for as long as 60 days, and Hopper covers up to $100 if the price rises during the freeze (checked on November 24, 2026).
  • The deposit is credited to your booking if you book; Hopper's help centre says the fee is non-refundable if you do not, and an unused freeze simply expires.
  • If the price falls during the freeze, Hopper's launch post says you pay the new lower price. The Points Guy wrote that "the savings aren't lucrative" for frequent travellers.
  • A freeze only protects the price you already found. Arbitrica, a personal AI agent, searches the same hotel from 195 countries for the lowest price in the world and keeps searching after you book until free cancellation ends.

Short answer

Hopper Price Freeze is worth it for a hotel only if you are fairly sure you will book that hotel through Hopper and you expect its price to rise by more than the deposit, up to Hopper's $100 cover. It does not find a lower price; it protects the one you already have. If what you want is the lowest price for the hotel, Arbitrica is the other route: a personal AI agent that searches the same hotel from 195 countries, on the site you are booking and other sites, checks that the room and final total match, and prepares the booking at the lowest price in the world for you to review and pay. Research across 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries found a lower price than the traveller's own in about 80% of countries, with a typical gap of 20-40%.

What Hopper Price Freeze does for hotels

According to Hopper's launch announcement (June 16, 2021), Hopper's help centre and The Points Guy (June 16, 2021), all checked on November 24, 2026:

  • Freeze a rate: you can freeze the best rate at a specific hotel for as long as 60 days, in the Hopper app on iOS and Android.
  • A deposit: you pay a Price Freeze deposit. The Points Guy reported that deposits vary with length of stay and price per night, with New York examples of $16 per night.
  • Cover if the price rises: if the price goes up during the freeze, Hopper covers up to $100 so you pay the earlier price.
  • If the price falls: you pay the new lower price, Hopper's launch post says.
  • The deposit: it is credited to your booking price if you book. Hopper's help centre says the fee is non-refundable and that an unused freeze expires at the end of its duration.

Hopper's own hotel terms that we could read today date from the 2021 launch; Hopper has not published a newer hotel Price Freeze explainer at the pages we checked, so the deposit, length and $100 cap may have changed. Hopper's homepage did not mention Price Freeze on November 24, 2026.

When a Price Freeze is not worth it

  • You might not book. The fee is non-refundable, so a freeze on a trip you drop is money spent.
  • The price falls or stays flat. You would pay the lower price anyway; the deposit bought protection you did not use.
  • A big rise. Cover stops at $100 on the booking, The Points Guy noted, so a larger rise is partly yours.
  • A lower price elsewhere. A freeze protects the Hopper price. Hopper's pages do not describe searching the same hotel from other countries (checked on November 24, 2026).
  • Verdicts. The Points Guy wrote that the feature "may not be a gamechanger" for frequent travellers. Going's Hopper guide (updated November 11, 2026) recommends Hopper "not be the only tool in your kit".

Hopper said customers saved on average $43 per stay with the freeze, according to The Points Guy. The gap between countries is often larger. In Arbitrica's own price check, a 4-night stay at White House Hotel Istanbul cost $1,066 searched from the UK and $708 searched from India, 33.6% less, for the same hotel and the same dates. Prices and room availability differ by country and by site, and dynamic pricing moves them through the day.

How Arbitrica differs

A freeze protects one price. Arbitrica is a personal AI agent that shows up after you choose your hotel room and goes after a lower one. For the hotel you have chosen, it:

  • searches the same hotel, on the same dates, from 195 countries, on the site you are booking and other sites;
  • checks that the hotel, room and conditions are identical and that the final total includes all taxes and fees;
  • finds the lowest price in the world for it and automatically prepares everything for the booking in a new tab, so you only review and pay;
  • keeps searching after you book until the free-cancellation window ends, as an approach Arbitrica uses, and confirms any new booking before the original is cancelled.

Price drops after booking often have to be acted on at once, before someone else takes the room at that rate, which is why a personal agent that acts instantly helps. Searching 195 countries by hand is humanly impossible; that is the job Arbitrica does. Research demonstrated an average of $47 per hotel booking with Arbitrica.
