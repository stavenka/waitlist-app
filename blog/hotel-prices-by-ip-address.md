# Do hotel prices change by IP address?

The same hotel can cost less when you search from another country. The reason is what each country has access to, not a price tag on your connection.

Search for the same hotel from two different countries and you can get two different prices. That leads many travelers to a simple theory: booking sites look at your IP address and charge you accordingly. The real picture is more useful. What changes the price you can get is mostly the country you search from, because different countries get access to different inventory, channels, local rates, promotions and currencies. Your IP address is simply how a search gets placed in a country.

## Do hotel prices change by IP address?

The short answer: what the evidence points to is the country you search from, not the connection itself. A search made from another country can reach offers that a search from your own country does not. Those offers can be for the very same hotel, on the same dates, with the same room, and they can be lower.

That distinction matters for what you do next. If prices were simply set by IP, any IP in any country would do. Because the difference comes from what each country has access to, the country you pick matters, the result varies from hotel to hotel, and the only way to find the lowest price is to search the same hotel from many countries and check that the offers are truly identical.

## Why the same hotel has a different price in a different country

A hotel rarely sells all its rooms through one price list. It distributes them through many routes, and many of those routes are tied to particular countries.

> Chart: Different countries get different inventory, channels, local rates, promotions and currencies, and pricing algorithms move rates all day.

- **Inventory.** Hotels allocate rooms to different partners, and some partners only sell in certain countries. A search from one country can see allocations a search from another country never reaches.

- **Channels.** Travel sites, wholesalers, tour operators and local agencies each have their own contracts with the hotel. The set of channels that answer a search differs by country.

- **Local rates.** Some rates are built for a domestic market, such as rates for residents of a country or for travelers booking in a local currency.

- **Promotions.** Sites and hotels run campaigns aimed at specific countries, and a promotion running in one market may not run in yours.

- **Currency.** A price quoted in another currency is converted at a rate set by the site or your card issuer, and that conversion can move the final total either way.

Put together, these routes mean the same hotel can come in at several prices at the same moment. The article on [hotel price differences by location](https://arbitrica.com/blog/hotel-price-discrimination-by-location.html) looks at this in more depth.

## What the research shows

In a test of 1,000 hotels searched from 100 countries, 80% of countries showed a lower price than the traveler's own country, and the typical gap was 20-40%.

An independent investigation points the same way. In April 2024, NordVPN commissioned an external agency to run simultaneous searches for identical travel products from the same vendors through servers in several countries. It reported that travel bookings made through US websites came out up to 70% higher than through websites elsewhere (its largest gap was a car rental). One example was a seven-night stay in Milan for a family of three: about $1,216 searched from the US and $1,012 searched from Italy. NordVPN sells VPNs, so it has an interest in the result, and the study covered a small set of specific bookings, but the pattern matches what travelers report.

The size of the gap changes with the hotel, the dates and the countries involved. Some hotels show almost no difference; others show a large one. ## Cookies, logged-in pricing and your device

Location is not the only thing that can change what you see. A well-known 2014 Northeastern University study tested 16 e-commerce and travel sites with real and simulated users. Among its travel findings, it reported that two travel sites offered reduced hotel prices to members, that some sites showed different results to some users, and that one site personalized results for mobile users. Another site reordered results based on users' click and purchase history, which the researchers did not count as price steering because the order did not track price.

Membership programs are the clearest case today. Booking.com's Genius program gives signed-in members discounts at participating properties, and Expedia's terms refer to Member Prices that are separate from prices available to the general public. If you compare a price while signed in with one while signed out, you are comparing two different offers.

TipTo test whether your own history affects what you see, run the same search in a private window and in your normal browser, with the same sign-in state. Treat member discounts as a separate offer, and compare totals with all taxes and fees included.

The study's travel findings were about member rates, devices and the order of results. They do not show a simple rule such as "the price goes up every time you search", so clearing cookies is a reasonable check but not a strategy for finding the lowest price.

## VPN hotel booking: is it cheaper?

A VPN lets you place a search in another country, so it can open up prices you cannot get from your own country. It is how most people first discover that the same hotel can cost less elsewhere. Doing it well takes care:

- **Start clean**Use a private window and the same sign-in state for every country you test.

- **Pick countries with a reason**Try the destination country and a few others. The lowest country changes from hotel to hotel.

- **Match the offer exactly**Same hotel, dates, guests, room type, meal plan and cancellation terms.

- **Compare the full total**Go to the final payment step and convert the currency yourself, including any card fees for paying in a foreign currency.

- **Read the rate conditions**Some rates are limited to residents of a country or to particular payment methods. NordVPN's own article notes that some sites prohibit VPN use in their terms.

The bigger problem is scale. A VPN checks one country at a time, and every check means a new search, a new currency and a new set of terms to verify. Covering a handful of countries takes an evening; covering all of them is not realistic by hand. ## What to remember about hotel prices and location

- The country you search from changes which offers you can reach: inventory, channels, local rates, promotions and currency.

- The same hotel is often 5-30% cheaper bought from another country or at another moment.

- Member rates and sign-in state are separate offers; keep them consistent when you compare.

- A lower price only counts if the hotel, room and terms are identical and the total includes all taxes and fees.
